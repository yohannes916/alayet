                                  Average Mass  
                         Objects do not need to have identical masses to be counted by weighing  
     All one needs to know is the average mass of the objects  
    To count the atoms in a sample of a given element by weighing, one must know the mass of the sample and the average mass for that element

                         Consider two samples containing different types of components, A and B

                                  Determining the Atomic Mass  
                         Atoms have tiny masses, so scientists created a unit to avoid using very small numbers  
       		1      atomic mass unit (     amu     )      = 1.66             10     –24      g   
    The average atomic mass for an element is the weighted average of the masses of all the isotopes of the element  
                     7   
              
                                  Average Atomic Mass for Carbon  
                         Even though natural carbon does not contain a single atom with a mass of 12.01, carbon is treated as though it is composed of only one type of atom with the same  mass  
    This enables counting atoms of natural carbon by weighing a sample of carbon

                                  Empirical Formula  
                         Formula of a compound that expresses the smallest whole-number ratio of the atoms present   
    The empirical formula can be found from the percent composition of the compound  
                     29   
              
                                  Formulas  
                         Empirical formula = CH  
     Simplest whole-number ratio  
    Molecular formula = (empirical formula)n 					        [n = integer]  
    Molecular formula = C     6     H     6      = (CH)     6  
     Actual formula of the compound  
                     30   
              
                                  Steps for Determining the Empirical Formula of a Compound  
                                A gaseous compound containing carbon and hydrogen was analyzed and found to consist of 83.65% carbon by mass. Determine the empirical formula of the compound.  
    Obtain the mass of each element present (in grams)  
    Assume you have 100 g of the compound  
       83.65% C = 83.65 g C   
       (100.00 – 83.65)  
       16.35% H = 16.35 g H   
                     31   
              
                                       Steps for Determining the Empirical Formula of a Compound      (continued 1)  
                          Determine the number of moles of each type of atom present  
             	   
                                                                              32   
              
                                       Steps for Determining the Empirical Formula of a Compound      (continued 2)  
                          Divide the number of moles of each element by the smallest number of moles to convert the smallest number to 1  
     If all the numbers obtained are integers (whole numbers), then these integers will be the subscripts in the empirical formula  
     If one or more of these numbers are not integers, proceed to the next step     
                                                                              33   
              
                                       Steps for Determining the Empirical Formula of a Compound      (continued 3)  
                          Multiply the numbers derived in the previous step by the smallest integer that will convert all of them to whole numbers  
     This set of whole numbers represents the subscripts in the empirical formula  
       
         	C: 1 × 3 = 3  
         	H: 2.33 × 3 = 7  
         	The empirical formula is C       3       H       7
