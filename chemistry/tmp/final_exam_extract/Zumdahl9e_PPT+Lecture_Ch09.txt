                                  Understanding the Molecular and Mass Information in a Balanced Equation  
                         Balanced chemical equation gives the amount of reactant and product molecules that participate in a chemical reaction  
    Coefficients in a balanced equation give the relative numbers of molecules  
              
                                  Understanding the Molecular and Mass Information in a Balanced Equation      (continued)  
                               
            C         2         H         5         OH                    +                    3O         2                                  2CO         2           +  3H         2         O   
     
     Equation is balanced  
     All atoms present in the reactants are accounted for in the products  
     1      molecule      of ethanol reacts with 3      molecules      of oxygen to produce 2      molecules      of carbon dioxide and 3      molecules      of water  
     1      mole      of ethanol reacts with 3      moles      of oxygen to produce 2      moles      of carbon dioxide and 3      moles      of water

                                  Steps for Calculating the Masses of Reactants and Products in Chemical Reactions  
                               
      Balance the equation for the reaction   
      Convert the masses of reactants or products to moles  
      Use the balanced equation to set up the appropriate mole ratio(s)  
      Use the mole ratio(s) to calculate the number of moles of the desired reactant or product  
      Convert from moles back to masses

                                  Stoichiometric Mixture  
                            Contains the relative amounts of reactants that match the numbers in a balanced equation  
          	       N       2       (       g       )  +  3H       2       (       g       )                    2NH       3       (       g       )        
                                            
                                  Limiting Reactant Mixture  
                               	       N       2       (       g       )  +  3H       2       (       g       )                    2NH       3       (       g       )        
                                            
                                  Limiting Reactant Mixture      (continued)  
                               	       N       2       (       g       )  +  3H       2       (       g       )                    2NH       3       (       g       )  
        
     Limiting reactant or reagent is the reactant that runs out first and thus limits the amounts of products that can form  
        H     2      is the limiting reactant for the reaction shown above   
              
                                  Limiting Reactants   
                         Determine which reactant is limiting to calculate the amounts of products that will be formed  
    Methane and water will react to form products according to the following equation:  
      
        			         CH         4          + H         2         O                     3H         2          + CO  
              
                                  Limiting Reactants      (continued 1)   
                                                       H     2     O molecules are used up first, leaving two unreacted CH     4      molecules  
              
                                  Limiting Reactants      (continued 2)   
                          Amount of products that can form is limited by the water  
     Water is the limiting reactant  
     Methane is in excess  
              
                                  Steps for Solving Stoichiometry Problems Involving Limiting Reactants by Solving for Limiting Reactant First (Approach A)  
                             Write and balance the equation for the reaction  
        Convert known masses of reactants to moles  
        Using the numbers of moles of reactants and the appropriate mole ratios, determine which reactant is limiting  
        Using the amount of the limiting reactant and the appropriate mole ratio, compute the number of moles of the desired product  
        Convert from moles of product to grams of product, using the molar mass, if this is required by the problem

                                  Percent Yield  
                          Important indicator of the efficiency of a particular laboratory or industrial reaction  
     Comparison of the actual yield of a product to its theoretical yield  
              
                                  Percent Yield      (continued 1)   
                             Theoretical Yield   
        The maximum amount of a given product that can be formed when the limiting reactant is completely consumed  
        The amount of product actually obtained   
    Actual yield, or the amount actually produced, of a reaction is usually less than the maximum expected, or theoretical yield
