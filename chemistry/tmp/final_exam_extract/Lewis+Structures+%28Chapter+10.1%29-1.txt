Octet Rule
• Atoms gain, lose or share electrons when bonding
 to fill the outer valence shell with 8 electrons.
• Hydrogen and Lithium only hold 2 electrons in their
 outer valence shell.
• Applies to nearly all period 2 elements and many
 others.

Lewis Structures
• A structural formula which consists of electron-dot
 symbols.
• Bonding pairs are indicated with a line
• Lone pairs are indicated as dots

Writing Lewis Structures
• Place atoms relative to each other
 • Atom with the lower group number is placed in the
  center. (CCl4)
  • Needs more electrons to attain an octet, usually has
    lower electronegativity.
  • If atoms are in the same group number, the atom with
    the higher period number goes in the center. (SO3)
  • Hydrogen is never a central atom since it only forms 1
    bond.

Writing Lewis Structures
• Draw a single line for each bonding pair from the
  central atom to an atom surrounding the central atom.
• Distribute the lone pairs of electrons so each atom
  ends up with 8 electrons.
  • Place lone pairs on surrounding atoms (more
    electronegative atoms first).
  • If electrons remain after the surrounding octets are
    full place the remaining electrons on the central
    atom.
  • Ex.
   • BF4-
   • NH4+

Resonance Structures
• Resonance is the delocalized sharing of electrons.
  • The electron pair is spread out over a few atoms, not just
  shared between 2.
• Resonance structure – one of two or more Lewis
 Structures for a species that cannot be depicted by a single
 structure.
 • These structures differ in the position of bonding and lone
   electron pairs only!!
   • Ex.
      • O3
      • NO2
 • The molecule is a resonance hybrid (average) of the
  resonance structures.                                          12

Exceptions to the Octet Rule
• Central Atom is an Electron-Deficient Atom
  • Molecules in which beryllium or boron is the
  central atom.
  • Fewer then 8 valence electrons
    • Ex.
      • BeCl2
      • BF3

 • React with other compounds to obtain an octet.
  • Ex.
    • BF3(g) + NH3(g)

Exceptions to the Octet Rule
• Central Atom can have an Expanded Valence Shell
 • Central Atom must be large and have empty orbitals
 • Occur in non-metals of Period 3 or higher!
 • Central Atom Bonds to more than 4 atoms.
   • Ex.
    • PCl5
    • SO42-

• Central Atom Bonds to fewer than 4 atoms
