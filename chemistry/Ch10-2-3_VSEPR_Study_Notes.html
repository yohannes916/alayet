1 The Core Idea — Electron Groups Repel Slides 2–3 · §10.2

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
VSEPR theoryValence-Shell Electron-Pair Repulsion: molecules minimize electron repulsions — each group of valence electrons around the central atom sits as far from the others as possible.Think of tied-together balloons: they naturally spread into the 5 arrangements below.
Electron groupONE "group" can be: a lone pair, a lone (single) electron, a single bond, a double bond, or a triple bond. A multiple bond counts as ONE group, not two or three.CO₂: two double bonds = 2 groups → linear.
Molecular shapeThe three-dimensional arrangement of nuclei joined by bonding groups. VSEPR is applied to a Lewis structure to find it.—
The 5 arrangementsMaximum mutual repulsion produces exactly five electron-group arrangements.2 groups → linear (180°) · 3 → trigonal planar (120°) · 4 → tetrahedral (109.5°) · 5 → trigonal bipyramidal (90°/120°) · 6 → octahedral (90°).

2 Electron-Group Arrangement vs Molecular Shape · AXmEn Slides 4–5

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Electron-group arrangementCounts ALL electron groups around the central atom — bonding groups AND lone pairs.H₂O has 4 groups (2 bonds + 2 lone pairs) → tetrahedral arrangement.
Molecular shapeDescribes only the positions of the nuclei — connected by bonding groups only. Lone pairs influence it but are invisible in the name.H₂O's shape is bent, not tetrahedral. Arrangement = shape only when there are no lone pairs.
AXmEn notationThe classification code: A = central atom, X = surrounding atom, E = non-bonding (lone) electron group; m and n are integers.H₂O = AX₂E₂; NH₃ = AX₃E; CH₄ = AX₄.

3 Bond Angles — Ideal vs Real Slides 6–7, 10

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Bond angleThe angle at the central atom's nucleus between the bonds to two surrounding atoms.—
Ideal angleThe pure-geometry angle of the arrangement. Observed when all bonding groups are identical and connect to the same type of atom (real = ideal).CH₄: all four C–H identical → exactly 109.5°.
When real ≠ idealAngles deviate when: ① a double bond is present; ② the surrounding atoms differ; ③ a lone pair is present.The three "real ≠ ideal" cases from slide 7.
Double-bond effectA double bond holds more electron density, so it repels single-bond pairs harder than they repel each other → it opens the angles next to it and squeezes the one opposite.Formaldehyde CH₂O: H–C=O opens to 122°, H–C–H closes to 116° (ideal was 120°).
Lone-pair effectLone pairs repel more strongly than bonding pairs — each lone pair added squeezes the bond angles smaller.The tetrahedral cascade: 109.5° (CH₄) → 107.3° (NH₃) → 104.5° (H₂O).

4 2 & 3 Electron Groups — Linear & Trigonal Planar Slides 8–10

ClassWhat to understandKey facts / examples
AX₂ — Linear2 electron groups point in opposite directions. Shape: linear, angle 180°.CS₂, HCN, BeF₂.
AX₃ — Trigonal planar3 groups point at the corners of an equilateral triangle. Angle 120°, all atoms in one plane.SO₃, BF₃, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻.
AX₂E — Bent (V-shaped)3 groups but one is a lone pair → the nuclei make a bent shape; the lone pair squeezes the angle below 120°.SO₂, O₃, PbCl₂, SnBr₂.

5 4 Electron Groups — the Tetrahedral Family Slides 11–14

ClassWhat to understandKey facts / examples
The arrangement4 groups point at the corners of a tetrahedron (a polyhedron of 4 equilateral-triangle faces). Ideal angle 109.5°. Every 4-group molecule uses this arrangement.NOT a flat square — the flat Lewis drawing lies about the 3-D shape.
AX₄ — TetrahedralAll 4 groups bonding. The go-to geometry of organic molecules.CH₄ (109.5°), SiCl₄, SO₄²⁻, ClO₄⁻.
AX₃E — Trigonal pyramidalOne lone pair (stronger repulsion) pushes the 3 bonds down → angle 107.3°.NH₃, PF₃, ClO₃⁻, H₃O⁺.
AX₂E₂ — Bent (V-shaped)Two lone pairs repel even harder → angle 104.5°.H₂O, OF₂, SCl₂.
NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺Beautiful proof of the lone-pair effect: protonating ammonia turns its lone pair into a bonding pair — the angle relaxes from 107.3° back to the ideal 109.5° and the shape becomes tetrahedral.NH₃ (trigonal pyramidal, 107.3°) → NH₄⁺ (tetrahedral, 109.5°).

6 5 Electron Groups — the Trigonal Bipyramidal Family Slides 15–20

ClassWhat to understandKey facts / examples
The arrangementTwo trigonal pyramids sharing a base. TWO kinds of positions: equatorial (3, on the triangle, 120° apart) and axial (2, above/below, 90° from the equatorial groups, 180° from each other).Only arrangement with two non-equivalent position types.
THE lone-pair ruleEquatorial–equatorial repulsions (120°) are weaker than axial–equatorial repulsions (90°). Since lone pairs repel hardest, lone pairs ALWAYS take equatorial positions.This single rule explains seesaw, T-shape, and linear below.
AX₅ — Trigonal bipyramidalAll bonding; ideal angles 120° (eq–eq) and 90° (ax–eq).PCl₅ (worked example), PF₅, AsF₅, SOF₄.
AX₄E — Seesaw1 lone pair, equatorial. Angles squeeze to 101.5° (eq) and 86.8° (ax).SF₄, XeO₂F₂, IF₄⁺, IO₂F₂⁻.
AX₃E₂ — T-shaped2 lone pairs, both equatorial. Ax–eq angle reduced to 86.2°.BrF₃ (worked example), ClF₃.
AX₂E₃ — Linear3 lone pairs fill the whole equatorial plane → the 2 bonded atoms sit axial, 180° apart.I₃⁻ (worked example), XeF₂, IF₂⁻.

7 6 Electron Groups — Octahedral Family · THE MASTER TABLE Slides 21–24

ClassWhat to understandKey facts / examples
The arrangementA polyhedron of 8 equilateral triangles and 6 identical vertices — all positions equivalent. Ideal angles 90°.—
AX₆ — OctahedralAll 6 groups bonding, 90° angles.SF₆ (worked example), IOF₅.
AX₅E — Square pyramidal1 lone pair → angles reduce to 81.9°.IF₅ (worked example), BrF₅, TeF₅⁻, XeOF₄.
AX₄E₂ — Square planar2 lone pairs sit opposite (trans) each other to minimize lone-pair–lone-pair repulsion → the 4 atoms make a flat square and angles stay 90° (each lone pair squeezes equally from both sides).XeF₄ (worked example), ICl₄⁻.

8 The 4-Step Shape Procedure · Multi-Center Molecules Slides 25–27

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
The 4 steps① Write the Lewis structure. ② Count ALL electron groups on the central atom → assign one of the 5 arrangements. ③ Predict the ideal angle and any deviation (lone pairs? double bonds?). ④ Count bonding vs non-bonding groups separately → name the shape (AXmEn) and draw it.Flow: Molecular formula → Lewis structure → arrangement → bond angles → molecular shape.
Worked exercise — SOF₄S is central with 5 groups (4 S–F bonds + 1 S=O double bond, which counts as ONE group) → trigonal bipyramidal, AX₅; the fat double bond takes an equatorial spot and pushes the real angles off the ideal 120°/90°.Slide-26 practice problem.
More than one central atomThe molecule's overall shape is a composite of the shape around EACH central atom, determined one center at a time.Ethane C₂H₆: two central C's, no lone pairs → two overlapping tetrahedra.

9 Molecular Polarity — Shape + Bond Polarity Slides 28–33 · §10.3

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Why polarity mattersA molecule's polarity influences its melting point, boiling point, solubility, reactivity, and biological function.The behavior chain: ΔEN → bond polarity → molecular polarity → intermolecular forces → physical properties / solubility / reactivity.
Polar bondA covalent bond between atoms that share electrons unequally (different electronegativities). The e⁻-rich end is δ−, the poor end δ+.H–F: δ+ on H, δ− on F.
Polar moleculeA molecule with an uneven charge distribution over the whole molecule. Polar molecules orient in an electric field — charged ends turn toward the oppositely charged plates (field off → random).HF between charged plates (slide 29).
Dipole moment (μ)The measure of molecular polarity. Unit: the debye (D); 1 D = 3.34 × 10⁻³⁰ C·m. Nonpolar molecule → μ = 0.—
THE key ideaPolar bonds do NOT guarantee a polar molecule. You must check whether the shape lets the bond dipoles cancel. Symmetric shape → dipoles cancel → nonpolar; asymmetric (lone pairs / different outer atoms) → net dipole → polar.Two-question test: ① any polar bonds? ② do they cancel?
CO₂ vs H₂OBoth have polar bonds (C 2.5 vs O 3.5; H 2.1 vs O 3.5). CO₂ is linear → the two dipoles pull in opposite directions and cancel → nonpolar. H₂O is bent → dipoles add → polar.The classic exam pair.
Same shape, different polarityCCl₄ and CHCl₃ are both tetrahedral. CCl₄ (4 identical C–Cl) is symmetric → nonpolar. Swap one Cl for H (CHCl₃, chloroform) → symmetry broken → polar.Cl EN = 3.0. Shape alone isn't enough — outer atoms must match too.
Practice trio (slide 31)NH₃: trigonal pyramidal, dipole toward N/lone pair → polar. BF₃: trigonal planar, symmetric → nonpolar. COS: linear like CO₂, but O ≠ S so the dipoles don't cancel → polar.—
cis vs trans (slide 32)Same formula, different polarity, different behavior: trans-1,2-dichloroethene → dipoles cancel, μ = 0 D, bp = 47.5 °C. cis isomer → dipoles add, μ = 1.90 D, bp = 60.3 °C. The polar isomer boils higher (stronger intermolecular forces).Polarity → measurable physical differences.

★ The One Flow to Anchor It All

MOLECULAR FORMULA │ ▼ step 1: draw the LEWIS STRUCTURE (10.1 recipe) │ ▼ step 2: count ALL e⁻ groups on central atom (multiple bond = 1 group) │ 2 → linear · 3 → trig planar · 4 → tetrahedral · 5 → trig bipyramidal · 6 → octahedral │ ▼ step 3: ideal angle → adjust: lone pair squeezes · double bond distorts │ 109.5° → 107.3° (1 lp) → 104.5° (2 lp) 5-group lone pairs go EQUATORIAL │ ▼ step 4: count bonding (X) vs lone (E) separately → AXmEn → SHAPE │ ▼ polarity: polar bonds? → do the dipoles cancel by symmetry? cancel → NONPOLAR (CO₂, BF₃, CCl₄) don't → POLAR (H₂O, NH₃, CHCl₃)

The two distinctions tested most:  ① Arrangement vs shape → count ALL groups vs describe only the nuclei (H₂O: tetrahedral arrangement, bent shape).  ② Polar bonds vs polar molecule → bonds can be polar while the molecule is nonpolar if the shape cancels the dipoles (CO₂, CCl₄, BF₃).

⭐ Highest-Yield — Drill These First

  1. The master table (§7) — all 13 AXmEn classes: groups → arrangement → shape → angle → one example each. This is the chapter.
  2. The angle cascade — 109.5° (CH₄) → 107.3° (NH₃) → 104.5° (H₂O): each lone pair squeezes harder. And NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺ restores 109.5°.
  3. Lone pairs go EQUATORIAL in trigonal bipyramidal (120° eq–eq repulsion < 90° ax–eq) — this generates seesaw, T-shaped, and linear.
  4. Square planar logic — in AX₄E₂ the two lone pairs sit trans, so angles stay 90°.
  5. Multiple bond = ONE electron group, but it repels harder (CH₂O: 122° / 116°).
  6. Polar bonds ≠ polar molecule — CO₂ vs H₂O; CCl₄ vs CHCl₃; BF₃ vs NH₃; COS. Two questions: polar bonds? do they cancel?
  7. Dipole moment — measured in debye, 1 D = 3.34 × 10⁻³⁰ C·m; polar molecules align in an electric field; cis (μ = 1.90 D, bp 60.3 °C) vs trans (μ = 0, bp 47.5 °C).
  8. The 4-step procedure — Lewis structure → count all groups → angles/deviations → AXmEn shape; composite shapes for multi-center molecules (ethane = 2 tetrahedra).
