1 Lewis (Electron-Dot) Symbols Slide 2 · review of 9.1

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Valence electronsFor a main-group element, the group number (1A–8A) = the number of valence electrons — and therefore how many dots the symbol gets.O is group 6A → 6 dots. Cl is 7A → 7 dots. C is 4A → 4 dots.
How to place the dotsAdd dots one at a time on each of the four sides of the symbol; only start pairing up after all four sides have one dot.Oxygen: 4 singles first, then pair up the 5th and 6th → two paired sides + two single dots.
Why it mattersThe dot symbol is the raw material of every Lewis structure — unpaired dots are where bonds form.O with 2 single dots → forms 2 bonds (H₂O!).

2 The Octet Rule Slide 3

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Octet ruleAtoms gain, lose, or share electrons when bonding to fill the outer valence shell with 8 electrons.Applies to nearly all Period 2 elements and many others.
The duet exceptionHydrogen and lithium hold only 2 electrons in their outer shell (a "duet," like He).H is satisfied by one bond — this is why H never needs lone pairs in structures.
Not universalThe octet rule has real exceptions — covered in §10.Be/B (too few), radicals (odd e⁻), Period 3+ (expanded).

3 What a Lewis Structure Shows Slide 4

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Lewis structureA structural formula made of electron-dot symbols: it shows every bond and every lone pair in a molecule.—
Bonding pairA pair of electrons shared between two atoms, drawn as a line.H–Cl : the line is 2 shared electrons.
Lone pairA pair of electrons on one atom only (non-bonding), drawn as two dots.In H–Cl, chlorine carries 3 lone pairs (6 dots) plus the bond → octet of 8.

4 Recipe Step 1 — Place the Atoms Slide 5

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Who goes in the centerThe atom with the LOWER group number goes in the center — it needs more electrons to reach an octet and usually has lower electronegativity.CCl₄: C (4A) is lower than Cl (7A) → C central, 4 Cl around it.
Same-group tiebreakIf the atoms are in the same group, the one with the higher period number (the bigger atom) goes in the center.SO₃: S and O are both 6A; S is Period 3 → S central.
Hydrogen ruleHydrogen is NEVER a central atom — it forms only 1 bond.H is always on the outside (terminal).

5 Recipe Steps 2–6 — The Five Counting Formulas Slides 6–8

QuantityFormula & meaningCCl₄ worked through
N — neededN = 8 × (# non-H atoms) + 2 × (# H atoms). Total electrons the molecule needs for every octet (2 for each H).5 non-H atoms → N = 8 × 5 = 40.
A — availableA = Σ valence electrons of every atom. For an ion: ADD 1 e⁻ per negative charge, SUBTRACT 1 e⁻ per positive charge.[1 × C(4e⁻)] + [4 × Cl(7e⁻)] = 32. (BF₄⁻: 3+28+1=32. NH₄⁺: 5+4−1=8.)
S — sharedS = N − A. The number of electrons that must be shared (in bonds) to satisfy everyone.S = 40 − 32 = 8.
bp — bonding pairsbp = S / 2. The number of bonds (lines) to draw.bp = 8/2 = 4 bonds.
lp — lone pairslp = A/2 − S/2 = A/2 − bp. The number of lone pairs left to place.lp = 32/2 − 4 = 12 lone pairs (3 on each Cl).

Memorize the chain: N → A → S = N − A → bp = S/2 → lp = A/2 − bp. Every Lewis-structure problem starts with these five numbers.

6 Recipe Steps 7–8 — Draw Bonds, Distribute Lone Pairs Slide 9

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Draw the bondsDraw a single line for each bonding pair from the central atom to each surrounding atom.CCl₄: 4 lines C→Cl.
Place lone pairs — order mattersDistribute lone pairs so each atom reaches 8: surrounding (more electronegative) atoms FIRST; any electrons left over go on the central atom.OF₂: fill both F first (3 lp each), remaining 2 pairs go on central O.
Worked ionsSame recipe works for polyatomic ions (after adjusting A for the charge). Bracket the ion and show its charge.BF₄⁻: 4 bonds, 3 lp per F. NH₄⁺: A = 8, S = 16−8 = 8 → 4 N–H bonds, lp = 4−4 = 0 lone pairs.
Practice set (slide 10)Run the full recipe yourself on the practice molecules.OF₂ (2 bonds, 8 lp) · CH₂Br₂ (4 bonds, 6 lp) · AlCl₄⁻ (4 bonds, 12 lp) · NH₃O hydroxylamine (4 bonds, 3 lp) · C₂H₆O dimethyl ether (8 bonds, 2 lp on O).

7 When Singles Aren't Enough — Multiple Bonds Slide 11

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
The triggerIf the central atom does not reach an octet with single bonds, convert a surrounding atom's lone pair into a shared pair → form one or more multiple bonds.The S = N − A math signals it: S is bigger than the number of single bonds can supply.
Double bond2 shared pairs (4 electrons), drawn as two lines.O₂: S = 16 − 12 = 4 → 2 pairs → O=O. N₂H₂: S = 20 − 12 = 8 → 4 bonds → H–N=N–H.
Triple bond3 shared pairs (6 electrons), drawn as three lines.CO: S = 16 − 10 = 6 → 3 pairs → C≡O (one lone pair on each atom).

8 Resonance Structures & Bond Order Slides 12–13

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
ResonanceThe delocalized sharing of electrons — an electron pair is spread out over several atoms, not just shared between 2.—
Resonance structureOne of two or more Lewis structures for a species that can't be captured by a single drawing. The structures differ ONLY in the positions of bonding and lone electron pairs — the atoms never move!O₃ (double bond on the left vs the right), NO₂.
Resonance hybridThe real molecule is a hybrid (average) of all its resonance structures — not flipping between them.Both O₃ bonds are identical, intermediate between single and double.
Bond orderThe number of electron pairs shared between a specific set of atoms. In resonance, partial (fractional) bonding appears: Bond order = # of shared electron pairs ÷ # of bonded-atom positions.CO₃²⁻: 4 bonding pairs spread over 3 C–O positions → bond order = 4/3 ≈ 1.33 (each C–O is between single and double).

9 Formal Charge & Picking the Major Resonance Structure Slides 14–15

ConceptWhat to understandKey facts / examples
Formal charge (FC)The hypothetical charge an atom would have if bonding electrons were shared equally. FC = valence e⁻ − (lone-pair e⁻ + ½ shared e⁻) — i.e. FC = V − (LP electrons + BP electrons/2).CO₃²⁻: C = 4 − (0 + 8/2) = 0; double-bond O = 6 − (4 + 4/2) = 0; each single-bond O = 6 − (6 + 2/2) = −1. Sum = −2 ✓ (must equal the ion's charge).
Sanity checkThe formal charges of all atoms must add up to the overall charge of the molecule (0) or ion.CO₃²⁻ → 0 + 0 + (−1) + (−1) = −2.
Major structure — 3 rulesThe major (most important) resonance structure has: (1) the smallest formal charges; (2) the same non-zero formal charges not on adjacent atoms; (3) the more negative FC on the more electronegative atom.NCO⁻: the structure N≡C–O⁻ (FC: N 0, C 0, O −1) wins — the −1 sits on O, the most electronegative atom. The version with −2 on N loses.

10 Exceptions to the Octet Rule — the Big 3 Slides 16–18

ExceptionWhat to understandKey facts / examples
① Electron-deficient central atomMolecules where beryllium or boron is central end up with fewer than 8 valence electrons on the central atom.BeCl₂ (4 e⁻ on Be), BF₃ (6 e⁻ on B). They react with e⁻-pair donors to complete the octet: BF₃ + NH₃ (N's lone pair fills B's octet).
② Odd number of electronsA molecule with an odd electron count must have one unpaired electron. It is paramagnetic and called a free radical — extremely reactive; attacks hydrogen in biomolecules, causing gene disruption.NO₂ (17 valence e⁻). Two radicals pair up: NO₂ + NO₂ → N₂O₄.
③ Expanded valence shellCentral atom holds MORE than 8 electrons. Requires a large atom with empty orbitals — nonmetals of Period 3 or higher (never Period 2!).Bonds to more than 4 atoms: PCl₅ (10 e⁻ on P), SO₄²⁻. Or fewer than 4 atoms but with extra lone pairs: SF₄ (4 bonds + 1 lone pair = 10 e⁻ on S).

★ The One Recipe to Anchor It All

LEWIS STRUCTURE RECIPE (drill until automatic) 1. PLACE ATOMS lower group # central · same group → higher period · H never central 2. N = 8×(non-H) + 2×(H) electrons NEEDED 3. A = Σ valence e⁻ (+1 per − charge, −1 per + charge) electrons AVAILABLE 4. S = N − A electrons SHARED 5. bp = S/2 → draw that many lines from the center 6. lp = A/2 − bp → outer atoms first, leftovers on center 7. Central atom short of 8? → convert a lone pair into a MULTIPLE BOND 8. Ion? Bracket it, show the charge. Check: FCs sum to the total charge. CCl₄: N=40 A=32 S=8 bp=4 lp=12 → 4 C–Cl bonds, 3 lone pairs per Cl

The two distinctions tested most:  ① Bonding pair vs lone pair → line (shared by 2 atoms) vs dots (owned by 1 atom).  ② N vs A → what the molecule needs vs what the atoms bring; their gap (S) is what must be shared.

⭐ Highest-Yield — Drill These First

  1. The five formulas — N = 8×(non-H) + 2×(H); A = Σ valence e⁻ (± charge); S = N − A; bp = S/2; lp = A/2 − bp. Run them cold on CCl₄ (40/32/8/4/12).
  2. Ion adjustment — add 1 e⁻ per negative charge, subtract 1 per positive (BF₄⁻ vs NH₄⁺).
  3. Central-atom rules — lower group number central; same group → higher period; H never central.
  4. Formal charge — FC = V − (LP e⁻ + BP e⁻/2); FCs must sum to the overall charge; worked on CO₃²⁻.
  5. Major resonance structure — smallest FCs, no same non-zero FCs adjacent, negative FC on the most electronegative atom (NCO⁻).
  6. Resonance ≠ atoms moving — only bonding/lone pairs move; the molecule is a hybrid. Bond order = shared pairs ÷ bonded positions (CO₃²⁻ = 4/3).
  7. The 3 octet exceptions — Be/B deficient (BeCl₂, BF₃); odd-electron free radicals (NO₂, paramagnetic); expanded shells only Period 3+ (PCl₅, SF₄, SO₄²⁻).
  8. Multiple-bond trigger — central atom short of an octet → double/triple bond (O₂, CO, N₂H₂).
