Lesson 9 — Signed Area: When the Graph Dips Below the Axis

Negative Rates, Net vs Total · Intuitive Calculus for a Curious Student · Lesson 9 of 20

📚 All lessons← Prev: Lesson 8Next: Lesson 10 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: understand what happens when a rate goes negative — the graph dips below the x-axis. You'll learn that the integral counts below-axis regions as NEGATIVE, why that's exactly the right behavior, and the crucial difference between net change and total.

1 A rate can be NEGATIVE

  • So far every rate in this course pointed one way: forward, filling, earning. But real rates go both directions:
  • Walking backwards: if forward speed is +3 mph, walking back toward home is a speed of −3 mph. The minus sign carries the direction.
  • Water draining: filling at 5 L/min is +5; draining at 5 L/min is −5 L/min — the tank's contents are changing at minus five liters per minute.
  • Money being spent: earning $20/day is +20; spending $20/day is −20 dollars/day.
  • Temperature dropping: warming 2° per hour is +2; cooling 2° per hour is −2 °C/hour.
  • On a graph, a negative rate is easy to spot: the curve dips BELOW the x-axis. Above the axis = gaining; below the axis = losing.

2 What accumulation means when the rate is negative

  • Our old rule was rate × time = amount accumulated. It still works — the minus sign just comes along: a rate of −5 L/min for 3 minutes accumulates (−5) × 3 = −15 liters. A LOSS of 15 liters.
  • Picture it: the region between a below-axis piece of graph and the x-axis is a perfectly ordinary shape — but the integral counts it as negative area, because it represents an amount lost, not gained.
  • So the honest rule is: the definite integral adds up SIGNED pieces — above-axis regions count plus, below-axis regions count minus, and the answer is the sum.
  • Nothing about the FTC changes! F(b) − F(a) automatically produces the correct signed answer. You don't do anything special; the minus signs take care of themselves.
Tiny example: a tank drains at a steady 4 L/min for 2 minutes. Rate graph: a flat line at height −4. The rectangle between the line and the axis is 2 wide and 4 tall — geometric size 8 — but it hangs BELOW the axis, so the integral is ∫02 (−4) dt = −8. Reading: the tank lost 8 liters. The sign is information, not an error.

3 NET vs TOTAL — the crucial pair of words

  • Walk forward 5 miles, then walk back 3 miles. Two honest questions with two different answers:
  • NET displacement: where are you compared to where you started? +5 + (−3) = +2 miles from home. This is what the plain integral gives you — it adds the signed pieces.
  • TOTAL distance walked: how far did your legs actually carry you? 5 + 3 = 8 miles. For this you add the SIZES of the pieces and ignore the signs.
  • Bank-account version: deposit $50, then withdraw $30. Net change in your balance: +$20. Total money that moved through the account: $80. Both numbers are true; they answer different questions.
  • Whenever a word problem asks about a rate that changes sign, your FIRST job is to decide: is this a NET question or a TOTAL question? Get that right and the math is easy.

4 Worked example — watching a below-axis piece cancel an above-axis piece

Geometry first: a toy robot's velocity is a steady −2 m/s from t = 0 to t = 3 (it's rolling backwards). The rate graph is a flat line below the axis; the rectangle is 3 wide, 2 tall → size 6, hanging below. So ∫03 (−2) dt = −6: the robot ends 6 meters BEHIND its start. FTC agrees: F(t) = −2t, and F(3) − F(0) = −6 − 0 = −6 ✓.
Now a rate that crosses the axis: evaluate ∫04 (x − 2) dx.

FTC routine: F(x) = x2/2 − 2x (check: derivative is x − 2 ✓).
F(4) = 16/2 − 8 = 8 − 8 = 0.   F(0) = 0.
Answer: 0 − 0 = 0. Zero?! The graph explains everything:
y = x − 2 2 | · | · ⟋ 1 | · ⟋ +2 ← above-axis triangle: area 2, counts PLUS | ⟋ 0 +---------·-------------------→ x | ⟋ 2 4 −1 | ⟋ −2 ← below-axis triangle: area 2, counts MINUS | ⟋ −2 · 0
  • From 0 to 2 the line is below the axis: a triangle of size ½ × 2 × 2 = 2, counted as −2. From 2 to 4 it's above: an identical triangle counted as +2. Signed sum: −2 + 2 = 0 — exactly what the FTC said.
  • The integral isn't broken. It faithfully reports the net: whatever was lost in the first half was regained in the second.

5 How to get the TOTAL (when you actually want it)

  • If the question asks for total distance, total water moved, total area of the regions — the recipe is:
  • Step 1: find where the rate crosses zero. Solve f(x) = 0. (For x − 2 that's x = 2.)
  • Step 2: integrate each piece separately, from limit to crossing, crossing to limit.
  • Step 3: add the ABSOLUTE VALUES (the sizes, signs stripped off).
Same example, total version: for x − 2 on [0, 4]: piece one ∫02 (x − 2) dx = (2 − 4) − 0 = −2; piece two ∫24 (x − 2) dx = 0 − (−2) = +2. Total area/total distance = |−2| + |+2| = 2 + 2 = 4. So: net 0, total 4. Both correct, different questions.

6 Reading a negative answer in a word problem

  • A negative integral is not a mistake — it's a sentence waiting to be read. Translate the sign using the story:
  • ∫ velocity = −6 → "ended 6 meters behind the start."
  • ∫ flow rate = −15 → "the tank has 15 liters less than before."
  • ∫ money rate = −40 → "spent $40 more than was earned."
  • ∫ temperature rate = −8 → "8 degrees colder than at the start."
  • Good final answers in word problems always convert the sign into a direction word: behind, less, colder, lost. Never leave a bare "−6" without saying what the minus means.

A remote-control car has velocity v = −3 feet per second (rolling backwards) from t = 0 to t = 4 seconds. Sketch the rate graph, then find ∫04 (−3) dt and say what it means.

Worked solution

Background: a negative velocity means moving backwards. Its graph is a flat line BELOW the x-axis, and the rectangle between the line and the axis counts as negative in the integral: signed area = −(width × height).
The graph is a flat line at height −3 from t = 0 to 4. The rectangle is 4 wide and 3 tall → geometric size 12, hanging below the axis.
Signed area = −12. FTC check: F(t) = −3t, F(4) − F(0) = −12 − 0 = −12 ✓.
Meaning: the car ends 12 feet BEHIND where it started.
−12 → the car is 12 feet behind its start
2.net vs total intro
A robot moves with velocity −5 m/s for 2 seconds. (a) What is its displacement (net change in position)? (b) What total distance did it travel?

Worked solution

Background: displacement is the SIGNED answer (where you ended up relative to the start — the integral's answer). Distance is the SIZE answer (how far the wheels rolled, signs ignored). When the velocity never changes sign, distance is just the absolute value of displacement.
(a) Displacement = rate × time = (−5) × 2 = −10 meters: it ends 10 m behind its start.
(b) The robot rolled steadily the whole time — its wheels covered |−10| = 10 meters.
(a) −10 m (10 m backward)   (b) 10 m
3.triangle below axis
Consider f(x) = −x from x = 0 to x = 4. (a) Describe the region between the graph and the x-axis and find its geometric size with the triangle formula. (b) Compute ∫04 (−x) dx with the FTC and compare.

Worked solution

Background: the line y = −x slopes downward below the axis, making a triangle that hangs under the x-axis. Geometry gives the SIZE of a shape (always positive); the integral gives the SIGNED version (negative here, since the shape is below the axis).
(a) The region is a triangle with base 4 (along the axis) and depth 4 (down to y = −4 at x = 4). Size = ½ × 4 × 4 = 8.
(b) FTC: F(x) = −x²/2 (check: derivative is −x ✓). F(4) = −16/2 = −8; F(0) = 0. Integral = −8 − 0 = −8.
Compare: same number, opposite sign — the integral counts this below-axis triangle as −8.
(a) size 8   (b) ∫ = −8, the signed version of the same triangle
4.draining tank
A tank is draining at a steady 2 liters per minute for 5 minutes. Write the rate as a signed number, compute the integral of the rate over the 5 minutes, and say what happened to the tank.

Worked solution

Background: "draining" means the amount of water is DECREASING, so the rate of change is negative: −2 L/min. Integrating a rate of change always gives the net change in the amount — sign included.
Rate = −2 L/min. Integral = ∫05 (−2) dt = (−2) × 5 = −10.
FTC check: F(t) = −2t, F(5) − F(0) = −10 − 0 = −10 ✓.

5.FTC · negative answer
Evaluate ∫02 (x − 3) dx and explain why the answer HAD to come out negative before you even computed it.

Worked solution

Background: on the interval [0, 2], the value of x − 3 runs from −3 up to −1 — it is negative the WHOLE time, so the graph stays below the axis and every piece of the accumulation is a loss. A negative answer is guaranteed.
F(x) = x²/2 − 3x (check: derivative is x − 3 ✓).
F(2) = 4/2 − 6 = 2 − 6 = −4.   F(0) = 0.
Integral = −4 − 0 = −4. As predicted: below the axis throughout → negative.
−4 (the graph never rises above the axis on [0, 2])
6.conceptual
Your friend says: "Negative area is nonsense — a rug can't have −8 square feet!" Is your friend right? Explain the difference between the area of a REGION and what the integral computes.

Worked solution

Background: the word "area" gets used two ways, and this question forces you to separate them. Geometric area (the size of a shape, like a rug) is a measure of stuff — it can't be negative. The integral computes something different: signed accumulation.
Your friend is right about rugs: the SIZE of any region is a positive number (or zero). No shape has negative size.
But the integral isn't measuring rug — it's adding up rate × time pieces, and those pieces can represent LOSSES. The minus sign on a below-axis region is bookkeeping: it records "this much was lost," using the region's size as the amount.
So "signed area" is a nickname for "accumulation that can go down as well as up" — not a claim that shapes have negative size.
Region size: never negative. Integral: signed accumulation, so it CAN be negative — the sign carries meaning (loss/backward), not geometry.
7.zero net · explain
Evaluate ∫06 (x − 3) dx with the FTC, then explain the answer using two triangles.

Worked solution

Background: the line y = x − 3 crosses the axis at x = 3: below the axis before that, above after. When the below-piece and above-piece have equal size, the signed sum is zero — the integral reports "no NET change," not "nothing happened."
FTC: F(x) = x²/2 − 3x (check: derivative is x − 3 ✓). F(6) = 36/2 − 18 = 18 − 18 = 0; F(0) = 0. Integral = 0 − 0 = 0.
Triangles: from 0 to 3, a below-axis triangle with base 3 and depth 3 → size ½ × 3 × 3 = 9/2, counted −9/2. From 3 to 6, an above-axis triangle of the same size, counted +9/2.
Signed sum: −9/2 + 9/2 = 0 ✓. Perfect cancellation.
0 — the below-axis triangle (size 9/2) exactly cancels the above-axis triangle (size 9/2)
8.net vs total · classic
A walker's velocity is v = 2 − t miles per hour on [0, 4] (forward at first, then backward). (a) Find the NET displacement ∫04 (2 − t) dt. (b) Find the TOTAL distance walked.

Worked solution

Background: v = 2 − t is positive before t = 2 (walking forward) and negative after (walking back). Net = one straight integral. Total = split at the crossing t = 2, integrate each piece, add the SIZES.
(a) F(t) = 2t − t²/2 (check: derivative is 2 − t ✓). F(4) = 8 − 16/2 = 8 − 8 = 0; F(0) = 0. Net = 0 − 0 = 0: the walker ends exactly where she started.
(b) Crossing: 2 − t = 0 at t = 2. Piece 1: F(2) − F(0) = (4 − 2) − 0 = +2 (two miles forward). Piece 2: F(4) − F(2) = 0 − 2 = −2 (two miles back).
Total distance = |+2| + |−2| = 2 + 2 = 4 miles.
(a) net 0 miles (back at the start)   (b) total 4 miles walked

Evaluate ∫03 (2x − 6) dx with the FTC, then check your answer with a triangle.

FTC: F(x) = x² − 6x (check: derivative is 2x − 6 ✓). F(3) = 9 − 18 = −9; F(0) = 0. Integral = −9 − 0 = −9.
Geometry: the triangle has base 3 (along the axis) and depth 6 (down to −6). Size = ½ × 3 × 6 = 9. Below the axis → counts as −9 ✓.
−9 (a below-axis triangle of size 9)
10.find crossing · split
For f(x) = x − 1 on [0, 3]: (a) find where the graph crosses the x-axis; (b) integrate each piece separately; (c) give the NET signed area and the TOTAL area of the regions.

Worked solution

Background: the full three-step "total" recipe: find the zero crossing (solve f(x) = 0), integrate each piece with the FTC, then choose — add signed pieces for NET, add absolute values for TOTAL.
(a) x − 1 = 0 at x = 1. Below the axis on [0, 1], above on [1, 3].
(b) F(x) = x²/2 − x (check: derivative is x − 1 ✓). Piece 1: F(1) − F(0) = (1/2 − 1) − 0 = −1/2. Piece 2: F(3) − F(1) = (9/2 − 3) − (1/2 − 1) = 3/2 − (−1/2) = 2.
(c) Net = −1/2 + 2 = 3/2. (Check straight through: F(3) − F(0) = 3/2 ✓.) Total = |−1/2| + |2| = 1/2 + 2 = 5/2.
(a) x = 1   (b) −1/2 and +2   (c) net 3/2, total 5/2
11.money · net vs total
You earn $15 per day for 4 days, then spend $10 per day for 3 days. (a) What is the net change in your money? (b) How much money moved in total (earned plus spent)?

Worked solution

Background: money has a signed rate too: earning is +15 dollars/day, spending is −10 dollars/day. The integral of the money rate = net change in your wallet. Total money moved = add the sizes of both flows.
Earning piece: (+15) × 4 = +60 dollars. Spending piece: (−10) × 3 = −30 dollars.
(a) Net = +60 + (−30) = +$30: you end $30 richer.
(b) Total moved = |60| + |−30| = 60 + 30 = $90 passed through your hands.
(a) net +$30   (b) $90 total moved
12.hiker · net vs total
A hiker walks away from camp at 4 mph for 2 hours, then walks back toward camp at 2 mph for 1 hour. (a) How far from camp does she end up (net)? (b) What total distance did she hike?

Worked solution

Background: pick a direction to call positive (away from camp). Then the two velocities are +4 mph and −2 mph, and the rate graph has a piece above the axis and a piece below. Net = signed sum; total = sum of sizes.
Away piece: (+4) × 2 = +8 miles. Back piece: (−2) × 1 = −2 miles.
(a) Net = +8 + (−2) = +6: she ends 6 miles from camp.
(b) Total hiked = |8| + |−2| = 8 + 2 = 10 miles of actual walking.
(a) 6 miles from camp   (b) 10 miles hiked
13.FTC · always below
Evaluate ∫02 (3x2 − 12) dx. Then explain how you could have predicted the SIGN of the answer just by looking at the integrand on [0, 2].

Worked solution

Background: before computing, test the integrand's sign: on [0, 2], 3x² is at most 3 × 4 = 12, so 3x² − 12 is never positive — the curve stays at or below the axis. Predicting the sign first is a powerful self-check on your final answer.
F(x) = x³ − 12x (check: derivative is 3x² − 12 ✓).
F(2) = 8 − 24 = −16.   F(0) = 0.
Integral = −16 − (0) = −16. Negative, as predicted — every moment on [0, 2] the quantity was falling.
−16 (integrand ≤ 0 on the whole interval, so the answer had to be negative)
14.tank · fill then drain
Water flows into a tank at the rate r(t) = 4 − 2t liters per minute for 0 ≤ t ≤ 4 (positive at first — filling; negative later — draining). (a) Find the net change ∫04 (4 − 2t) dt. (b) Find the total amount of water that moved in or out. (c) Tell the tank's story in one sentence.

Worked solution

Background: the rate crosses zero when 4 − 2t = 0, at t = 2: filling before, draining after. Net = one integral straight through. Total moved = split at t = 2, add sizes.
(a) F(t) = 4t − t² (check: derivative is 4 − 2t ✓). F(4) = 16 − 16 = 0; F(0) = 0. Net = 0 − 0 = 0.
(b) Piece 1: F(2) − F(0) = (8 − 4) − 0 = +4 (four liters in). Piece 2: F(4) − F(2) = 0 − 4 = −4 (four liters out). Total moved = 4 + 4 = 8 liters.
(c) Story: the tank gained 4 liters during the first 2 minutes, lost those same 4 liters during the last 2 minutes, and ended exactly where it began.
(a) net 0 L   (b) 8 L moved   (c) filled 4, drained 4, back to the start
15.curve · net and total
A particle's velocity is v(t) = t2 − 4 m/s on [0, 3]. (a) Where does v cross zero? (b) Find the net displacement. (c) Find the total distance traveled. Leave fractions in your answers.

Worked solution

Background: first curved-graph net-vs-total problem — but the recipe is identical to the straight-line ones: solve v = 0, use the FTC on each piece (F(t) = t³/3 − 4t), then signed sum for net, sizes for total. Expect thirds in the arithmetic.
(a) t² − 4 = 0 at t = 2 (only the positive root matters here). Negative before t = 2 (moving backward), positive after.
(b) F(t) = t³/3 − 4t (check: derivative is t² − 4 ✓). Net = F(3) − F(0) = (27/3 − 12) − 0 = 9 − 12 = −3 meters: the particle ends 3 m behind its start.
(c) Piece 1: F(2) − F(0) = (8/3 − 8) − 0 = 8/3 − 24/3 = −16/3. Piece 2: F(3) − F(2) = −3 − (−16/3) = −9/3 + 16/3 = 7/3.
Total = |−16/3| + |7/3| = 16/3 + 7/3 = 23/3 meters. (Sanity: net −16/3 + 7/3 = −9/3 = −3 ✓ matches part b.)
(a) t = 2   (b) net −3 m (3 m behind the start)   (c) total 23/3 m
16.temperature · warm then cool
A greenhouse's temperature changes at the rate r(t) = 3 − t degrees per hour for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5. (a) Find the net temperature change over the 5 hours. (b) Find the total number of degrees the temperature moved (up plus down).

Worked solution

Background: a temperature-change rate is positive while warming, negative while cooling; here the switch happens when 3 − t = 0, at t = 3. Net change = straight integral; total movement = split at t = 3 and add sizes. F(t) = 3t − t²/2.
(a) F(t) = 3t − t²/2 (check: derivative is 3 − t ✓). Net = F(5) − F(0) = (15 − 25/2) − 0 = 30/2 − 25/2 = 5/2. The greenhouse ends 5/2 degrees warmer.
(b) Piece 1 (warming, 0 to 3): F(3) − F(0) = (9 − 9/2) − 0 = 9/2. Piece 2 (cooling, 3 to 5): F(5) − F(3) = 5/2 − 9/2 = −2.
Total moved = |9/2| + |−2| = 9/2 + 4/2 = 13/2 degrees. (Sanity: 9/2 + (−2) = 5/2 ✓ matches part a.)
(a) net +5/2 degrees (warmer)   (b) 13/2 degrees of total movement
17.conceptual · settle the argument
Two students compute ∫04 (x − 2) dx = 0 for a walker whose velocity is x − 2. Student A says "the answer is 0, so the walker never moved." Student B says "the walker definitely moved." Who is right? Use net vs total to settle it.

Worked solution

Background: a zero integral is one of the most misread results in calculus. The integral reports NET change — where you ended relative to where you began. It says nothing about how much motion happened along the way.
Student B is right. The velocity is negative on [0, 2] (walking backward) and positive on [2, 4] (walking forward) — the walker was moving almost the entire time.
The pieces: ∫02 = −2 and ∫24 = +2. Net displacement = −2 + 2 = 0 (ends at the start). Total distance = 2 + 2 = 4 (four units of real walking).
Correct reading of "integral = 0": the walker ENDED where she started — not that she never moved.
Student B. Net displacement is 0, but total distance walked is 4 — a zero integral means "back where you started," not "motionless."
18.solve for the limit
Find the number b > 0 that makes ∫0b (x − 2) dx = 0. Then explain with triangles why YOUR b is exactly the right stopping point.

Worked solution

Background: now the unknown is the LIMIT of integration. Strategy: run the FTC with the letter b in place of a number, set the result equal to 0, and solve the equation. This "integrate first, solve second" move shows up constantly in later math.
F(x) = x²/2 − 2x, so ∫0b (x − 2) dx = F(b) − F(0) = b²/2 − 2b.
Set it to zero: b²/2 − 2b = 0 → b(b/2 − 2) = 0 → b = 0 or b = 4. Since we need b > 0: b = 4.
Triangle explanation: below-axis loss from 0 to 2 is a triangle of size 2. The above-axis gain grows after x = 2, and by x = 4 the above triangle has size exactly 2 — the books balance precisely at b = 4. Stop sooner and the loss wins; later and the gain wins.
b = 4 — the moment the +2 triangle above the axis exactly repays the −2 triangle below
19.bank account · zero net
Money flows into a savings account at $20 per week for 3 weeks (deposits), then out at $30 per week for 2 weeks (withdrawals). (a) What does the integral of the money rate over all 5 weeks equal, and what does it tell you? (b) How much money moved through the account in total? (c) Why are these two answers so different?

Worked solution

Background: the money rate is +20 dollars/week, then −30 dollars/week — a step graph with a piece above the axis and a piece below. The integral adds signed pieces (net change in balance); total money moved adds their sizes.
(a) Deposits: (+20) × 3 = +60. Withdrawals: (−30) × 2 = −60. Integral = +60 + (−60) = 0: the balance ends exactly where it started.
(b) Total moved = |+60| + |−60| = 60 + 60 = $120 passed through the account.
(c) They answer different questions: the integral tracks the BALANCE (net), which came back to its start; the total tracks ACTIVITY, and there was $120 worth of it. Zero net, busy account.
(a) 0 — balance unchanged   (b) $120 moved   (c) net measures where you ended; total measures how much happened
20.challenge · everything at once
A drone flies along a straight track with velocity v(t) = 6 − 2t meters per second on [0, 5]. (a) When does it turn around? (b) Find its net displacement. (c) Find the total distance flown. (d) Write a one-sentence story of the flight, translating every sign.

Worked solution

Background: the full toolkit in one problem: find the zero crossing (turnaround = where velocity changes sign), FTC for the net, split-and-add-sizes for the total, and a signed-number translation at the end. F(t) = 6t − t².
(a) 6 − 2t = 0 at t = 3 seconds. Forward before t = 3, backward after — that's the turnaround.
(b) F(t) = 6t − t² (check: derivative is 6 − 2t ✓). Net = F(5) − F(0) = (30 − 25) − 0 = +5 meters.
(c) Piece 1: F(3) − F(0) = (18 − 9) − 0 = +9 (nine meters out). Piece 2: F(5) − F(3) = 5 − 9 = −4 (four meters back). Total = |9| + |−4| = 13 meters.
(d) Sanity: 9 + (−4) = 5 ✓ matches part b.
(a) t = 3 s   (b) net +5 m   (c) total 13 m   (d) the drone flew 9 m out, turned around at t = 3, flew 4 m back, and ended 5 m from its launch point having flown 13 m in all
