Lesson 13 — Real-World Accumulation Problems

One pattern, many costumes: water, money, bacteria, weather · Lesson 13 of 20

📚 All lessons← Prev: Lesson 12Next: Lesson 14 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: realize that the motion problems you've mastered were never really about motion. Whenever ANYTHING flows, arrives, or grows at a known rate, the total is an integral. Today you'll meet the same math wearing four costumes — water, money, bacteria, and weather — and learn to read a word problem like a detective.

1 The universal pattern

  • Every rate problem in this course has secretly been the same sentence: if something changes at a rate r(t), then the TOTAL change from time a to time b is ∫ab r(t) dt.
  • Velocity was just one example — nothing about motion is special. Liters per minute, dollars per day, bacteria per hour, millimeters of rain per hour: anything with a "per" is a rate, and integrating a rate gives a total.
rate of change r(t) --integrate--> total accumulated speed (m/s) --∫--> distance (m) flow (L/min) --∫--> water (L) earnings ($/day) --∫--> money ($) growth (bugs/hour) --∫--> new bacteria (bugs) rainfall (mm/hour) --∫--> rain depth (mm)
  • And just like position needed a starting value: if there was already some amount at the start, then amount at time b = starting amount + ∫ of the rate.

2 Costume 1 — water and liquids

  • A rate like r(t) = 6t liters per minute means the flow is getting stronger over time: at t = 1 the water pours in at 6 L/min, at t = 2 it's 12 L/min, and so on — like slowly opening a faucet.
Worked example: a tank fills at r(t) = 6t L/min. How much water enters during the first 4 minutes?
∫04 6t dt = [3t²]04 = 48 − 0 = 48 liters.
Units: (L/min) × min = L ✓. And if the tank already held 10 L at the start, it now holds 10 + 48 = 58 liters — starting amount plus integral.

3 Costume 2 — money

  • Money accumulates at a rate too. Suppose a lemonade stand earns at the rate r(t) = 50 + 10t dollars per day — $50/day at first, and growing by $10/day each day as word spreads.
  • Total earned over the first week: ∫07 (50 + 10t) dt = [50t + 5t²]07 = 350 + 245 = 595 → $595.
  • Business people have their own names for this rate — "revenue rate" or "cash flow" — but it's the same object: dollars per day, waiting to be integrated.

4 Costume 3 — biology (growing populations)

  • A colony of bacteria might grow at the rate r(t) = 3t² bacteria per hour — slow at first, then faster and faster as more bacteria are around to divide.
  • New bacteria gained in the first 4 hours: ∫04 3t² dt = [t³]04 = 64 bacteria.
  • Population = starting population + integral. If the dish started with 200 bacteria: 200 + 64 = 264 bacteria after 4 hours.

5 Costume 4 — weather and the environment

  • Rainfall: a weather station reports rain rate in mm per hour. Integrate over the storm → total rainfall depth in your rain gauge.
  • Pollution: a factory pipe leaks chemicals into a lake at some liters-per-hour rate. Integrate → total liters of pollution in the lake. (This is how environmental scientists estimate spills.)
  • Snow melting: melting is a rate OUT — the snow pile's height loses the integral: height = starting height − ∫ (melt rate).
  • New words, same machine. When you see a new field's jargon, hunt for the "per" — that's your r(t).

6 Reading a word problem like a detective

  • Four things to spot, in order:
    • ① The rate: the quantity with a "per" in its units (L per min, $ per day...). That's r(t).
    • ② The window: the "from ... to ..." times. Those are your limits a and b.
    • ③ A starting amount: does the story mention something already there ("the tank already holds 30 L")? If so, add it after integrating.
    • ④ In minus out: is something flowing in AND out at once? Then the thing only feels the NET rate = in-rate − out-rate (Lessons 1 and 9).
Fully worked in-and-out example: a pool fills from a hose at a steady 8 L/min, but it has a leak that drains water at 2 + t L/min (the leak worsens over time).

① Rates: in = 8, out = 2 + t. ④ Net rate: 8 − (2 + t) = 6 − t L/min.
Read the net rate: it's positive until t = 6 (pool gaining), zero at t = 6, negative after (leak wins — pool draining!). The pool is fullest at t = 6.
Water gained in the first 6 minutes: ∫06 (6 − t) dt = [6t − ½t²]06 = 36 − 18 = 18 liters.
After t = 6 the level starts dropping — the same "signed area" story as a ball past its peak in Lesson 12.
✏️ Exercises — 20 problems (write your answer on paper, then reveal)
1.water
Water flows into a tank at the rate r(t) = 4t liters per minute. How much water enters during the first 5 minutes?

Worked solution

Background: "liters per minute" is a rate, so the total water is the integral of the rate over the time window. The antiderivative of 4t is 2t² (check: the derivative of 2t² is 4t ✓).
Total = ∫05 4t dt = [2t²]05 = 2 × 25 − 0 = 50.
Units: (L/min) × min = liters ✓.
50 liters
2.money
A dog-walking business earns money at a steady rate of 20 dollars per day. Use an integral to find the total earned in 6 days.

Worked solution

Background: a constant rate is the easiest integral — it's just rate × time, the rectangle from Lesson 1. Writing it as an integral shows the pattern: total = ∫ (dollars per day) over the days.
Total = ∫06 20 dt = [20t]06 = 120.
Sanity check: $20/day for 6 days = 20 × 6 = 120 ✓ — the integral agrees with common sense.
$120
3.water
A bathtub fills at the rate r(t) = 5 + 2t liters per minute. How much water enters in the first 4 minutes?

Worked solution

Background: integrate each piece separately: the antiderivative of the constant 5 is 5t, and the antiderivative of 2t is t². A rate like 5 + 2t means "starts at 5 L/min and strengthens by 2 L/min every minute".
Total = ∫04 (5 + 2t) dt = [5t + t²]04 = 20 + 16 = 36.
36 liters
4.biology
A bacteria colony grows at the rate r(t) = t² bacteria per hour. How many NEW bacteria appear during the first 3 hours?

Worked solution

Background: "bacteria per hour" is a growth rate; integrating it counts the total new arrivals. The antiderivative of t² is t³/3 (check: derivative of t³/3 is t² ✓).
New bacteria = ∫03 t² dt = [t³/3]03 = 27/3 = 9.
9 new bacteria
5.weather
During a storm, rain falls at the rate r(t) = 6 − t millimeters per hour (heavy at first, easing off). How much rain falls between t = 0 and t = 4 hours?

Worked solution

Background: rainfall rate (mm per hour) tells how fast the water depth in a rain gauge rises. A decreasing rate like 6 − t means the storm weakens — but it's still raining as long as the rate is positive (it stays positive until t = 6, so our whole window is fine).
Total rain = ∫04 (6 − t) dt = [6t − ½t²]04 = 24 − 8 = 16.
16 millimeters of rain
6.starting amount
A tank already holds 30 liters. Water flows in at a steady 5 liters per minute for 8 minutes. How much water is in the tank at the end?

Worked solution

Background: the integral only counts water that arrives DURING the window — it knows nothing about what was already there. Final amount = starting amount + integral of the rate. (Same reason velocity problems needed v₀.)
Water added = ∫08 5 dt = 5 × 8 = 40 liters.
Final amount = 30 + 40 = 70 liters.
70 liters
7.interpret
Water enters a reservoir at the rate r(t) liters per minute, where t is in minutes. In plain English, what does ∫25 r(t) dt mean in this story, and what are its units?

Worked solution

Background: an "interpret the integral" question doesn't want a number — it wants the MEANING. Recipe: the integral of a rate over a window = the total amount accumulated during that window. The limits tell you the window; the rate's units divided by "per what" tell you the answer's units: (L/min) × min = L.
The window is from minute 2 to minute 5.
So the integral is the total number of liters of water that entered the reservoir between t = 2 and t = 5 minutes.
The water (in liters) added between minute 2 and minute 5
8.money
A summer lemonade stand earns at the rate r(t) = 50 + 10t dollars per day. Find the total earned over the first week (t = 0 to t = 7).

Worked solution

Background: a growing earnings rate — $50/day at the start, climbing $10/day each day (maybe good reviews are spreading). Businesses call r(t) a "revenue rate"; math calls it a function to integrate.
Total = ∫07 (50 + 10t) dt = [50t + 5t²]07.
= 50 × 7 + 5 × 49 = 350 + 245 = 595.
$595
9.reverse · find the time
A tank starts empty and fills at the rate r(t) = 4t liters per minute. At what time does the tank reach 50 liters?

Worked solution

Background: a reverse problem — the total is given, the TIME is unknown. Strategy: write the amount as a formula of t (integrate up to an unknown time t), set it equal to the target, and solve. Same trick as "how long to fall 80 m" in Lesson 12.
Amount at time t = ∫0t 4u du = 2t² liters.
Set 2t² = 50 → t² = 25 → t = 5.
Check: 2 × 5² = 50 ✓.
At t = 5 minutes
10.in minus out
Water pours into a barrel at 10 L/min while a crack leaks water out at 3 + t L/min. (a) Write the net rate. (b) How much water does the barrel gain in the first 4 minutes? (c) At what time does the barrel stop gaining?

Worked solution

Background: with flow in AND out, the barrel only feels the NET rate = in − out. While the net rate is positive the barrel gains; when it hits zero the level peaks; after that the barrel loses. This is exactly the peak-of-the-thrown-ball logic from Lesson 12.
(a) Net rate = 10 − (3 + t) = 7 − t L/min.
(b) Gain = ∫04 (7 − t) dt = [7t − ½t²]04 = 28 − 8 = 20 liters.
(c) Net rate = 0 when 7 − t = 0 → t = 7 minutes. After that the leak wins.
(a) 7 − t L/min   (b) 20 liters   (c) at t = 7 minutes
11.biology · with start
A dish starts with 200 bacteria, and the colony grows at r(t) = 3t² bacteria per hour. What is the population after 4 hours?

Worked solution

Background: two ingredients: the starting population (already there — the integral doesn't know about it) plus the new arrivals (the integral of the growth rate). The antiderivative of 3t² is t³.
New bacteria = ∫04 3t² dt = [t³]04 = 64.
Population = 200 + 64 = 264.
264 bacteria
12.two-phase
A hose fills a kiddie pool at 6 L/min for the first 5 minutes, then someone turns it down to 2 L/min for the next 5 minutes. How much water enters in total?

Worked solution

Background: when the rate follows different rules on different stretches, split the integral at the change (Lesson 6's splitting property): ∫₀¹⁰ = ∫₀⁵ + ∫₅¹⁰, using the right formula on each piece.
Phase 1: ∫05 6 dt = 6 × 5 = 30 liters.
Phase 2: ∫510 2 dt = 2 × 5 = 10 liters.
Total = 30 + 10 = 40 liters.
40 liters
13.weather · melting
A snow pile is 60 cm tall when the sun comes out. It melts at the rate r(t) = 2t centimeters per hour (melting speeds up as the day warms). How tall is the pile after 5 hours?

Worked solution

Background: melting is a rate OUT, so it subtracts: height = starting height − ∫ (melt rate). Compute the total melted, then take it away from what was there.
Total melted = ∫05 2t dt = [t²]05 = 25 cm.
Height = 60 − 25 = 35 cm.
35 centimeters tall
14.reverse · with start
A tank already holds 20 liters and fills at the rate r(t) = 6t liters per minute. At what time does the tank hold 95 liters?

Worked solution

Background: combine two skills: amount = start + integral, and solve-for-the-time. Write the amount formula first, THEN set it equal to the target — don't forget the starting 20 is part of the 95.
Amount at time t = 20 + ∫0t 6u du = 20 + 3t².
Set 20 + 3t² = 95 → 3t² = 75 → t² = 25 → t = 5.
Check: 20 + 3 × 25 = 95 ✓.
At t = 5 minutes
15.two-phase · mixed formulas
Water enters a tank at r(t) = 4t L/min for the first 3 minutes; after t = 3 the flow locks at a constant 12 L/min. How much water enters from t = 0 to t = 8?

Worked solution

Background: the rate has two different FORMULAS, so split the integral at t = 3. Notice the rate is continuous: 4t at t = 3 equals 12, exactly where the constant phase picks up — the faucet stops opening wider and holds steady.
Phase 1: ∫03 4t dt = [2t²]03 = 18 liters.
Phase 2: ∫38 12 dt = 12 × (8 − 3) = 60 liters.
Total = 18 + 60 = 78 liters.
78 liters
16.compare scenarios
Business A earns at a steady 40 dollars per day. Business B starts from nothing but grows: it earns at 10t dollars per day. Over the first 10 days, which business earns more, and by how much?

Worked solution

Background: comparing two accumulations = computing two integrals and subtracting. Graphically, A's rate is a flat line at 40 and B's is a ramp reaching 100 by day 10; the winner is whoever encloses more AREA, not whoever ends with the higher rate.
A: ∫010 40 dt = 400 dollars.
B: ∫010 10t dt = [5t²]010 = 500 dollars.
B wins by 500 − 400 = 100 dollars — even though B earned almost nothing the first few days.
Business B earns more, by $100
17.in minus out · peak
A pond holds 10 kiloliters of water. A stream feeds it at 8 kL/hour while evaporation and outflow remove water at 2t kL/hour. (a) When is the pond fullest? (b) How much water does it hold at that moment?

Worked solution

Background: net rate = in − out = 8 − 2t. The pond rises while the net rate is positive and peaks the instant it hits zero — exactly like a thrown ball peaking when v = 0. Peak amount = start + integral of the net rate up to the peak time.
(a) 8 − 2t = 0 → t = 4 hours. Before t = 4 the net rate is positive (filling); after, negative (draining).
(b) Gain by t = 4: ∫04 (8 − 2t) dt = [8t − t²]04 = 32 − 16 = 16 kL.
Peak amount = 10 + 16 = 26 kL.
(a) fullest at t = 4 hours   (b) 26 kiloliters
18.weather · rise and fall
A storm's rain rate is r(t) = 6t − t² millimeters per hour, for t from 0 to 6 hours (it builds up, peaks, then dies out — the rate is 0 at both t = 0 and t = 6). How much total rain falls during the whole 6-hour storm?

Worked solution

Background: a realistic storm: the rate rises, peaks (at t = 3, where r = 9 mm/h), and fades. Integrate each piece: the antiderivative of 6t is 3t², and of t² is t³/3. The rate stays ≥ 0 on the whole window, so the integral is straight accumulation — no signed-area worries.
Total = ∫06 (6t − t²) dt = [3t² − t³/3]06.
= 3 × 36 − 216/3 = 108 − 72 = 36.
36 millimeters of rain
19.challenge · reverse biology
A dish starts with 50 bacteria, and the colony grows at r(t) = 3t² bacteria per hour. After how many hours does the population reach 175?

Worked solution

Background: the full detective kit in one problem: population = start + integral, target given, time unknown. Build the population formula, set it to 175, solve. A cube root appears — look for a number whose cube you know.
Population at time t = 50 + ∫0t 3u² du = 50 + t³.
Set 50 + t³ = 175 → t³ = 125 → t = 5 (since 5 × 5 × 5 = 125).
Check: 50 + 125 = 175 ✓.
After 5 hours
20.challenge · full pool story
A pool holds 100 liters. A hose adds water at 12 L/min while a widening leak drains it at 2t L/min. (a) When is the pool fullest, and how much does it hold then? (b) At what time is the pool back down to its starting 100 liters?

Worked solution

Background: net rate = 12 − 2t: positive until t = 6, negative after. The pool's whole story: amount(t) = 100 + ∫₀ᵗ (12 − 2u) du = 100 + 12t − t². Part (b) is the "ball lands back where it started" question — the gained area above the axis is exactly cancelled by lost area below it (Lesson 9's signed area).
(a) Fullest when net rate = 0: 12 − 2t = 0 → t = 6 min. Amount = 100 + 12 × 6 − 6² = 100 + 72 − 36 = 136 liters.
(b) Back to the start when 12t − t² = 0 → t(12 − t) = 0 → t = 12 minutes.
Note the symmetry: 6 minutes filling up, 6 minutes draining back — just like the thrown ball's mirror-image flight in Lesson 12.
(a) fullest at t = 6 min with 136 liters   (b) back to 100 L at t = 12 min
