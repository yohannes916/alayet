Lesson 14 — The Average Value of a Function

What "average temperature all day" really means · Intuitive Calculus for a Curious Student · Lesson 14 of 20

📚 All lessons← Prev: Lesson 13Next: Lesson 15 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: learn what the average value of a changing quantity means, and how to compute it: average = (total accumulated) ÷ (length of the interval). The integral finds the total — then one division finishes the job.

1 The everyday question

  • The weather report says: "the average temperature today was 62°F." But the temperature changed every instant — it was one thing at 6 AM, another at noon, another at 4 PM. What can "average" possibly mean here?
  • You know how to average a list of numbers: add them up, divide by how many there are. Your test average = (sum of scores) ÷ (number of tests).
  • But a temperature graph has infinitely many values — one for every instant of the day. You can't add up infinitely many numbers one at a time, and you can't divide by "infinity many"…
  • …or can you? You already own a tool whose whole job is adding up infinitely many tiny contributions: the integral. This lesson puts it to work.

2 Build it from a trick you already know

  • Back in Lesson 1 you met average speed: if a trip covers 90 miles in 2 hours, the average speed is 90 ÷ 2 = 45 mph. In words: average rate = total distance ÷ total time.
  • Notice what that recipe needs: a total (which the integral computes) divided by a length of time. Nothing in the recipe cares that it's about speed — it works for ANY changing quantity.
  • So here is the definition, for any function f on an interval from a to b:
The average value formula:
average value of f from a to b = (total accumulated) ÷ (length of interval) = (1/(b−a)) ∫ab f(x) dx

Two steps, always the same: Step 1 — integrate f from a to b (that's the "adding up infinitely many values" part). Step 2 — divide by the interval's length, b − a.
  • Why does dividing by the length make sense? Because the integral counts value × width for every skinny slice. Dividing the grand total by the total width tells you the value per unit of width — exactly what "average" should mean.

3 The picture: water settling flat

  • Here's the picture to keep forever. The region under the graph of f is like water in a wavy container — tall in some places, short in others.
  • Now let the water settle flat. Same water, same area — but now it's a rectangle with the same width, b − a. The height of that flat water level is the average value.
the wavy water… …settles flat 9 | · 9 | | · | 6 | · 6 | | · | height = 3 | · area = 9 3 | █████████████ AVERAGE = 3 | · | █ area = 9 █ 0 +·—————————————→ x 0 +——————————————→ x 0 3 0 3
  • So the average value is the height of the rectangle that has the same area as the region under f. That's why the formula is area ÷ width: for a rectangle, height = area ÷ width.
  • Check it makes sense: the flat level must sit below the peaks and above the valleys of the graph — an average is always between the biggest and smallest values.

4 Worked example: f(x) = x² on [0, 3]

Find the average value of f(x) = x² from x = 0 to x = 3.

Step 1 — the total: ∫03 x2 dx = x3/3 evaluated from 0 to 3 = 27/3 − 0 = 9.
Step 2 — divide by the length: the interval runs from 0 to 3, so its length is 3. Average = 9 ÷ 3 = 3.
  • Plausibility check: on this interval, f runs from f(0) = 0 up to f(3) = 9, so the average had to land between 0 and 9. ✓
  • But notice: the average is 3, which is below the midpoint 4.5. Why? Look at the graph of x² — it stays low for a long time and only shoots up near the end. The curve spends more of its time at small values, so the average leans low. A nice honesty check: the answer isn't just legal, it makes sense.

5 Worked example with motion: average velocity

A cart's velocity is v(t) = 6t m/s from t = 0 to t = 4 seconds. Find its average velocity.

Step 1 — total distance: ∫04 6t dt = 3t2 evaluated from 0 to 4 = 48 − 0 = 48 meters.
Step 2 — divide by the time: average velocity = 48 ÷ 4 = 12 m/s.
  • Quick sanity check: the velocity starts at v(0) = 0 and ends at v(4) = 24, and (0 + 24)/2 = 12. It matches!
  • But careful — that endpoint shortcut worked only because v = 6t is a straight line. A straight line climbs at a perfectly steady pace, so it spends equal time above and below its middle. Curves don't play fair like that.
  • The integral formula works for everything — lines, curves, wiggles, all of it. The endpoint trick is a special-case coincidence.

6 The classic mistake (don't fall for it!)

  • The tempting shortcut: "average of f from a to b = (f(a) + f(b))/2." Usually wrong! It only looks at the two endpoints and ignores everything the function did in between.
  • Watch it fail: for f(x) = x2 on [0, 3], the endpoints give (0 + 9)/2 = 4.5. But we computed the true average: 3. The shortcut overshot because x² hugs the ground early on, and the endpoints can't see that.
  • The rule: endpoint-averaging is only guaranteed for straight lines. For anything curved, you must integrate — the integral is the only method that listens to every single value along the way.
✏️ Exercises — 20 problems (write your answer on paper, then reveal)
1.sanity check
Find the average value of the constant function f(x) = 7 on the interval [0, 10]. Before computing, predict the answer!

Worked solution

Background: the average value of f on [a, b] is (1/(b−a)) ∫ab f(x) dx — total accumulated, divided by the interval's length. A constant function never changes, so its average should be… itself. If the formula is any good, it must agree.
Prediction: a function that is always 7 should average to 7.
Step 1 (total): ∫010 7 dx = 7x from 0 to 10 = 70.
Step 2 (divide by length): interval length = 10 − 0 = 10. Average = 70 ÷ 10 = 7. The formula passes the sanity check!
Average value = 7
2.straight line
Find the average value of f(x) = 2x on [0, 4]. Then check your answer by averaging the two endpoint values.

Worked solution

Background: average value = total ÷ length. The total comes from the integral, using the power rule backwards: an antiderivative of 2x is x². For straight lines (and ONLY straight lines), the endpoint average (f(a)+f(b))/2 gives the same answer — a handy way to double-check.
Step 1: ∫04 2x dx = x2 from 0 to 4 = 16 − 0 = 16.
Step 2: length = 4 − 0 = 4. Average = 16 ÷ 4 = 4.
Endpoint check: f(0) = 0 and f(4) = 8, so (0 + 8)/2 = 4. ✓ It agrees, because 2x is a straight line.
Average value = 4
3.straight line
Find the average value of f(x) = x + 3 on [0, 6]. Check with the endpoint average.

Worked solution

Background: to integrate a sum, integrate each piece: an antiderivative of x is x²/2, and an antiderivative of 3 is 3x. Then average = total ÷ length as always.
Step 1: ∫06 (x + 3) dx = x2/2 + 3x from 0 to 6 = (18 + 18) − 0 = 36.
Step 2: length = 6. Average = 36 ÷ 6 = 6.
Endpoint check: f(0) = 3, f(6) = 9, and (3 + 9)/2 = 6. ✓ (straight line, so the shortcut is allowed).
Average value = 6
4.reverse problem
The average value of f on [0, 4] is 5. What is the total, ∫04 f(x) dx?

Worked solution

Background: the formula average = total ÷ length can be run backwards: total = average × length. This is just like "average speed 5 mph for 4 hours means 20 miles total" — you don't need to know the details of f at all!
total = average × length = 5 × (4 − 0) = 5 × 4 = 20.
Picture version: the settled water level is at height 5 across a width of 4, so the water's area is 5 × 4 = 20 — and that's the area under f too.
∫04 f(x) dx = 20
5.curve
Find the average value of f(x) = x² on [0, 6].

Worked solution

Background: same two steps for curves as for lines — the formula doesn't care about the shape. Power rule backwards: an antiderivative of x² is x³/3.
Step 1: ∫06 x2 dx = x3/3 from 0 to 6 = 216/3 − 0 = 72.
Step 2: length = 6. Average = 72 ÷ 6 = 12.
Plausibility: f runs from 0 up to f(6) = 36, and 12 is between them — but well below the midpoint 18, because x² spends most of its time low. Makes sense.
Average value = 12
6.average velocity
A scooter's velocity is v(t) = 10t m/s from t = 0 to t = 6 seconds. Find its average velocity, and check it with the endpoint trick (allowed here — why?).

Worked solution

Background: average velocity = (total distance) ÷ (total time) — the original Lesson 1 idea. The integral of velocity gives total distance (Lesson 11). The endpoint shortcut (v(start)+v(end))/2 is legal only because v = 10t is a straight line.
Step 1: distance = ∫06 10t dt = 5t2 from 0 to 6 = 180 meters.
Step 2: average velocity = 180 ÷ 6 = 30 m/s.
Endpoint check: v(0) = 0, v(6) = 60, (0 + 60)/2 = 30 ✓ — allowed because the velocity graph is a straight line.
Average velocity = 30 m/s
7.cubic
Find the average value of f(x) = x³ on [0, 2].

Worked solution

Background: power rule backwards: an antiderivative of x³ is x⁴/4 (raise the power by one, divide by the new power). Then divide the total by the interval length.
Step 1: ∫02 x3 dx = x4/4 from 0 to 2 = 16/4 − 0 = 4.
Step 2: length = 2. Average = 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
Plausibility: f runs from 0 to f(2) = 8, and the average 2 is well below the midpoint 4 — x³ hugs the ground even harder than x² does, so a low average is exactly right.
Average value = 2
8.polynomial
Find the average value of f(x) = 3x² on [0, 4].

Worked solution

Background: a constant multiplier rides along for free: an antiderivative of 3x² is 3 · (x³/3) = x³. (Differentiate x³ to check: you get 3x². ✓)
Step 1: ∫04 3x2 dx = x3 from 0 to 4 = 64 − 0 = 64.
Step 2: length = 4. Average = 64 ÷ 4 = 16.
Average value = 16
9.polynomial
Find the average value of f(x) = x² + 1 on [0, 3].

Worked solution

Background: integrate term by term: antiderivative of x² is x³/3, antiderivative of 1 is x. Interesting shortcut to notice afterwards: f is just "x² lifted up by 1," so its average should be the average of x² on [0,3] (which the lesson computed: 3) lifted up by 1.
Step 1: ∫03 (x2 + 1) dx = x3/3 + x from 0 to 3 = (9 + 3) − 0 = 12.
Step 2: length = 3. Average = 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
Check with the shortcut idea: average of x² on [0,3] is 3, plus the lift of 1 → 4. ✓
Average value = 4
10.temperature story
Starting at 6 AM (call that t = 0), the temperature in a greenhouse is T(t) = 2t + 50 degrees Fahrenheit, where t is in hours. What is the average temperature from 6 AM to 6 PM (t = 0 to t = 12)?

Worked solution

Background: this is the weather-report question from the start of the lesson! "Average temperature" over a stretch of time means exactly: integrate the temperature function over the interval, then divide by how long the interval is.
Step 1: ∫012 (2t + 50) dt = t2 + 50t from 0 to 12 = 144 + 600 = 744.
Step 2: length = 12 hours. Average = 744 ÷ 12 = 62.
Check: T is a straight line from T(0) = 50 up to T(12) = 74, and (50 + 74)/2 = 62 ✓.
Average temperature = 62°F
11.average speed
A drone's velocity is v(t) = 3t² m/s from t = 0 to t = 4 seconds. (a) How far does it travel? (b) What is its average velocity?

Worked solution

Background: total distance = the integral of velocity (Lesson 11). Average velocity = that total ÷ the elapsed time. Since v = 3t² is a curve, do NOT expect the endpoint trick to work.
(a) ∫04 3t2 dt = t3 from 0 to 4 = 64 meters.
(b) Average velocity = 64 ÷ 4 = 16 m/s.
Compare: the endpoint trick would say (v(0) + v(4))/2 = (0 + 48)/2 = 24 — too big! The curve spends most of the trip slow, so the true average (16) is lower.
(a) 64 meters   (b) average velocity = 16 m/s
12.reverse problem
The average value of f on the interval [2, 7] is 10. What is ∫27 f(x) dx?

Worked solution

Background: total = average × length. Careful with the length when the interval doesn't start at 0: it's (end − start), not just the end value. This is the same "width = end − start" care you used with rectangle widths back in Lesson 1.
Length = 7 − 2 = 5 (not 7!).
Total = average × length = 10 × 5 = 50.
∫27 f(x) dx = 50
13.shifted interval
Find the average value of f(x) = x² on the interval [1, 4].

Worked solution

Background: two places to be careful when the interval starts at 1 instead of 0: the definite integral needs BOTH endpoints plugged in (top value minus bottom value, from the Fundamental Theorem), and the length is b − a = 4 − 1 = 3.
Step 1: ∫14 x2 dx = x3/3 from 1 to 4 = 64/3 − 1/3 = 63/3 = 21.
Step 2: length = 4 − 1 = 3. Average = 21 ÷ 3 = 7.
Plausibility: f(1) = 1 and f(4) = 16, and 7 sits between them (below the midpoint 8.5, as x² curves demand).
Average value = 7
14.conceptual
Your friend says: "To average f(x) = x² on [0, 3], I'll just average the ends: (0 + 9)/2 = 4.5. Done!" Explain, in your own words, why the true average (3) is smaller — and for what kind of function your friend's method WOULD be correct.

Worked solution

Background: the endpoint average only sees two values and assumes the function climbed at a steady pace between them. The integral sees every value. When a function lingers at low values, its true average leans low — no two-point shortcut can detect lingering.
The graph of x² stays close to the ground for most of [0, 3] — for example, all the way out at x = 1.5 (half the interval!) it has only reached 2.25 out of 9. It saves its big values for a short sprint at the end.
An average is a time-share: values you spend MORE time near count for more. Since x² spends most of the interval low, the true average (3) is below the endpoint midpoint (4.5).
The friend's method is correct exactly when the function climbs at a perfectly steady rate — a straight line. Lines spend equal time above and below their middle, so the two endpoints tell the whole story.
x² lingers low, so its average sits below 4.5; endpoint-averaging only works for straight lines
15.find c
The average value of f(x) = x² on the interval [0, c] is 12. Find c.

Worked solution

Background: now the interval itself is the unknown. Strategy: write the average-value formula with c left as a letter, simplify, then solve the resulting equation. You'll need ∫0c x2 dx = c3/3 — the same power-rule computation as always, just with c at the top.
Total: ∫0c x2 dx = x3/3 from 0 to c = c³/3.
Average = (c³/3) ÷ c = c²/3.
Set it equal to 12: c²/3 = 12 → c² = 36 → c = 6 (take the positive answer, since the interval runs forward from 0).
Check: on [0, 6], total = 216/3 = 72, average = 72/6 = 12 ✓.
c = 6
16.find c
The average value of f(x) = 4x on the interval [0, c] is 10. Find c.

Worked solution

Background: same strategy as the last problem: express the average in terms of c, then solve. Bonus check available here: 4x is a straight line, so the average should also equal (f(0) + f(c))/2 = (0 + 4c)/2 = 2c.
Total: ∫0c 4x dx = 2x2 from 0 to c = 2c².
Average = 2c² ÷ c = 2c.
Set 2c = 10 → c = 5.
Check: on [0, 5], total = 2 × 25 = 50, average = 50/5 = 10 ✓. And the endpoint shortcut agrees: (0 + 20)/2 = 10 ✓.
c = 5
17.rectangle picture
Consider f(x) = 6x − x² on [0, 6] (an arch that starts at 0, peaks at height 9 when x = 3, and comes back to 0). (a) Find its average value. (b) Describe the rectangle that has the same area as the region under the arch. (c) Does your answer sit sensibly between the arch's lowest and highest values?

Worked solution

Background: the average value IS the height of the equal-area rectangle — the "settled water level." A settled level must sit between the region's lowest point and its peak: some of the arch pokes above the level, and exactly enough gap sits below it to balance.
(a) Total: ∫06 (6x − x2) dx = 3x2 − x3/3 from 0 to 6 = 108 − 72 = 36. Average = 36 ÷ 6 = 6.
(b) The rectangle has width 6 (the whole interval) and height 6 (the average), so its area is 36 — exactly the area under the arch. If the arch were water, it would settle to a flat level at height 6.
(c) The arch runs from height 0 (at the ends) up to 9 (at the middle), and 6 is between 0 and 9 ✓. The arch pokes above the level near the middle and dips below near the ends — the extra and the missing balance perfectly.
(a) average = 6   (b) a 6-wide, 6-tall rectangle, area 36   (c) yes: 0 < 6 < 9
18.average rate story
Water pours into a tank at the rate r(t) = t² liters per minute, from t = 0 to t = 6 minutes. (a) How much water enters in total? (b) What is the average fill rate? (c) Check: if a plain faucet ran at that constant average rate for 6 minutes, would it deliver the same total?

Worked solution

Background: integrating a flow rate gives the total amount delivered (Lesson 13). The average rate is the one CONSTANT rate that would deliver the same total in the same time — that's the real meaning of "average": a fair stand-in.
(a) Total = ∫06 t2 dt = t3/3 from 0 to 6 = 216/3 = 72 liters.
(b) Average rate = 72 ÷ 6 = 12 liters per minute.
(c) A constant 12 L/min for 6 minutes delivers 12 × 6 = 72 liters — the same total ✓. The wavy inflow and the steady faucet fill the tank equally.
(a) 72 liters   (b) 12 L/min   (c) yes: 12 × 6 = 72 ✓
19.two-piece journey
A robot's velocity is v(t) = 2t m/s from t = 0 to t = 3 seconds, and then a constant 6 m/s from t = 3 to t = 5 seconds. Find its average velocity over the whole trip [0, 5].

Worked solution

Background: when a function is described in pieces, integrate each piece separately and add the totals (chop–compute–add, the Lesson 1 move). Then divide by the FULL interval length — the average belongs to the whole trip, not to either piece.
Piece 1: ∫03 2t dt = t2 from 0 to 3 = 9 meters.
Piece 2: constant rate → 6 m/s × 2 s = 12 meters. (Total time there: 5 − 3 = 2 seconds.)
Whole trip: 9 + 12 = 21 meters in 5 seconds. Average velocity = 21 ÷ 5 = 4.2 m/s.
Average velocity = 4.2 m/s
20.challenge · average + net change
A particle's velocity is v(t) = 3t² − 2t m/s from t = 0 to t = 3 seconds. (a) Find the net change in the particle's position. (b) Find its average velocity. (c) Verify your two answers fit together: a particle moving at the constant average velocity for 3 seconds should end up with the same net change.

Worked solution

Background: two big ideas meet here. Net change in position = the integral of velocity (Lesson 12: the FTC says the integral of a rate equals the change in the total). Average velocity = that same integral ÷ elapsed time. So (a) and (b) share one integral, and (c) is the water-settling story told with motion.
(a) ∫03 (3t2 − 2t) dt = t3 − t2 from 0 to 3 = 27 − 9 = 18. Net change = 18 meters.
(b) Average velocity = 18 ÷ 3 = 6 m/s.
(c) Constant 6 m/s for 3 seconds → 6 × 3 = 18 meters ✓. The real, changing motion and the steady stand-in motion produce the identical net change — that's exactly what "average" promises.
(a) 18 meters   (b) 6 m/s   (c) 6 × 3 = 18 ✓
