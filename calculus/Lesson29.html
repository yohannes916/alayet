Lesson 29 — Integral Properties & Symmetry

The Rulebook & the Mirror Trick · Intuitive Calculus for a Curious Student · Lesson 29 of 40

📚 All lessons← Lesson 28Next: Lesson 30 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: collect the official rulebook of definite integrals — add, scale, split, reverse — and learn to combine given integral values like puzzle pieces. Then meet even and odd functions, whose mirror symmetry lets you evaluate whole integrals with almost no computation at all. This is one of the AP exam's favorite topics, and it's mostly about seeing pictures.

1 The rulebook, collected

  • You've quietly used most of these rules since Lesson 1. Today they become official, because from now on we'll combine them constantly. Each rule has a one-line picture-reason — if you can see the picture, you never need to memorize the formula.
RuleIn symbolsPicture-reason
Sums split up∫ab (f + g) dx = ∫ab f dx + ∫ab g dxstack one graph on top of the other: total area = area + area
Constants slide out∫ab c·f dx = c·∫ab f dxa shape stretched 3× taller has exactly 3× the area
Intervals split∫ab f dx = ∫ac f dx + ∫cb f dxcut the region with one vertical line: piece + piece = whole
Walking backwards∫ba f dx = −∫ab f dxsweep the region right-to-left and every little width dx turns negative
Zero width∫aa f dx = 0a region with width 0 has area 0
  • Surprise about the splitting rule: the cut point c does not have to sit between a and b! For example ∫24 f dx = ∫29 f dx + ∫94 f dx — the second integral walks backwards from 9 to 4, un-counting the extra area. The algebra always balances.

2 Using given values — the AP staple

  • A hugely common exam setup: you're told a few integral values (no formula for f at all!) and asked to compute new ones. It's pure rulebook algebra — like solving a little puzzle.
Worked example 1: given ∫05 f dx = 12 and ∫03 f dx = 7, find ∫35 f dx.
Splitting says: ∫05 = ∫03 + ∫35, so 12 = 7 + ∫35 f dx → ∫35 f dx = 5. (The whole minus the first chunk leaves the second chunk.)
Worked example 2: given ∫28 f dx = 10, find ∫82 3f dx.
Two rules in a row: constant out, then reverse the direction.
∫82 3f dx = 3·∫82 f dx = 3·(−∫28 f dx) = 3·(−10) = −30.

3 Even & odd functions, from scratch

  • A function is even if f(−x) = f(x) — plugging in −x gives the same output. The graph is a mirror image across the y-axis. Examples: x² (since (−3)² = 9 = 3²), cos x, |x|.
  • A function is odd if f(−x) = −f(x) — plugging in −x flips the sign of the output. The graph has 180° rotation symmetry through the origin: spin the page half a turn and the graph lands on itself. Examples: x, x³ (since (−2)³ = −8 = −(2³)), sin x.
  • The algebraic test: compute f(−x) carefully and compare. If it simplifies to f(x) → even. If it simplifies to −f(x) → odd. If neither → neither (most functions are neither!).
  • Handy shortcut for powers: xeven power is even, xodd power is odd — that's where the names come from. Constants count as even (a horizontal line is mirror-symmetric).
Worked test: is f(x) = x³ + x even, odd, or neither?
f(−x) = (−x)³ + (−x) = −x³ − x = −(x³ + x) = −f(x). Sign flipped exactly → odd.

4 The symmetry shortcuts

  • Symmetry pays off when the interval is symmetric too: from −a to a. Then:
  • Odd function on [−a, a]: the integral is 0. The area on the left is a perfect upside-down copy of the area on the right — they cancel completely.
ODD (like x³): left piece cancels right piece y | _╱ | ╱ +A (area above axis) ───────┼──────────→ x −a ╱ | a ╱‾ | −A (area below axis) | total = (−A) + (+A) = 0 … no computing needed!
  • Even function on [−a, a]: the integral is 2 × ∫0a. The left half is an exact mirror of the right half — so compute one half and double it.
EVEN (like x²): mirror image across the y-axis y ╲ | ╱ ╲ | ╱ ╲__|__╱ A | A ← two identical areas ───────┼──────────→ x −a 0 a total = A + A = 2 × (area from 0 to a)
Worked (odd): ∫−22 x³ dx = 0 because x³ is odd on a symmetric interval.
Verify by FTC: antiderivative is x⁴/4, so we get (2)⁴/4 − (−2)⁴/4 = 4 − 4 = 0 ✓. The shortcut and the honest computation agree.
Worked (even): ∫−33 x² dx = 2·∫03 x² dx = 2·[x³/3]03 = 2·9 = 18. Half the work, same answer as integrating straight across.

5 Mixed integrands — split by term, use the right rule on each

  • Most integrands are neither purely even nor purely odd — but linearity lets you split them term by term and hit each term with the right shortcut.
Worked: ∫−11 (x³ + x² + 1) dx.
· x³ is odd → its integral over [−1, 1] is 0.
· x² is even → 2·∫01 x² dx = 2·(1/3) = 2/3.
· 1 is even (a constant) → ∫−11 1 dx = 2 (a 2-wide, 1-tall rectangle).
Total: 0 + 2/3 + 2 = 8/3. The odd term simply vanished — that's the whole trick.

6 Comparison sense-checks — catching your own mistakes

  • Two common-sense facts make great error detectors:
  • If f ≥ 0 everywhere on [a, b], then ∫ab f dx can't be negative. An accumulation of non-negative amounts can't come out below zero. If your answer for ∫03 (x² + 1) dx is negative, you made a sign slip — no need to re-check anything else first.
  • Bigger function → bigger integral: if f(x) ≥ g(x) on the whole interval, then ∫ab f dx ≥ ∫ab g dx. A taller graph traps more area. Use it for quick bounds: since x² + 1 > x² on [0, 3], its integral must beat ∫03 x² dx = 9.
  • Get in the habit: after every integral, ask "does the sign and rough size make sense?" Thirty seconds of sense-checking saves whole problems on a test.
✏️ Exercises — 20 problems (write your answer on paper, then reveal)
1.given values · split
You are told ∫06 f dx = 9 and ∫02 f dx = 4. Find ∫26 f dx.

Worked solution

Background: the splitting rule says the whole region from 0 to 6 is the chunk from 0 to 2 plus the chunk from 2 to 6. Knowing the whole and one chunk, subtraction gives the other chunk — no formula for f needed.
Split: ∫06 f dx = ∫02 f dx + ∫26 f dx.
Plug in the givens: 9 = 4 + ∫26 f dx, so ∫26 f dx = 9 − 4 = 5.
∫26 f dx = 5
2.reversal · zero width
Given ∫15 f dx = 8, find (a) ∫51 f dx and (b) ∫44 f dx.

Worked solution

Background: swapping the limits means sweeping the region right-to-left, which flips the sign of the answer. And an integral whose two limits are equal covers a region of width zero — area 0, always, for any function.
(a) ∫51 f dx = −∫15 f dx = −8.
(b) ∫44 f dx = 0 — zero width, zero area. We don't even need the given value.
(a) −8   (b) 0
3.linearity
Given ∫03 f dx = 10 and ∫03 g dx = 3, find (a) ∫03 (f + g) dx and (b) ∫03 5f dx.

Worked solution

Background: linearity is the "stack and stretch" pair of rules. Stacking g's graph on top of f's adds their areas; stretching f five times taller multiplies its area by 5.
(a) ∫03 (f + g) dx = ∫03 f dx + ∫03 g dx = 10 + 3 = 13.
(b) ∫03 5f dx = 5·∫03 f dx = 5·10 = 50.
(a) 13   (b) 50
4.classify
Use the algebraic test (compute f(−x)) to decide: is f(x) = x⁴ − 1 even, odd, or neither?

Worked solution

Background: the test is mechanical: replace every x by −x, simplify, and compare to the original. Even powers of x destroy the minus sign; odd powers keep it.
f(−x) = (−x)⁴ − 1 = x⁴ − 1, because (−x)⁴ = x⁴ (four minus signs multiply to a plus).
That's exactly f(x) again, so the function is even. (Graph check: x⁴ − 1 is a mirror-symmetric bowl shifted down 1.)
Even
5.classify
Even, odd, or neither: f(x) = x³ + x? Show the f(−x) test.

Worked solution

Background: a sum of odd terms stays odd — every term flips sign, so the whole expression flips sign. Watch that happen in the algebra.
f(−x) = (−x)³ + (−x) = −x³ − x.
Factor out the minus: −x³ − x = −(x³ + x) = −f(x). The output flipped sign exactly → odd.
Odd
6.odd shortcut + FTC check
Evaluate ∫−44 x dx using symmetry, then verify your answer with the FTC.

Worked solution

Background: f(x) = x is the simplest odd function — its graph is a line through the origin, making two identical triangles: one below the axis (left), one above (right). On a symmetric interval they cancel perfectly.
Symmetry: x is odd and the interval [−4, 4] is symmetric, so the integral is 0.
FTC check: antiderivative x²/2. Evaluate: (4)²/2 − (−4)²/2 = 8 − 8 = 0 ✓.
Picture check: triangle below the axis has area 8 (counts −8), triangle above has area 8 (counts +8). Sum: 0 ✓.
0
7.classify · trig
Classify sin x and cos x as even or odd, using the facts sin(−x) = −sin x and cos(−x) = cos x. Explain what each fact says about the graph.

Worked solution

Background: these two identities come straight from the unit circle: walking backwards (angle −x) flips your height (sine) but keeps your horizontal position (cosine). They are exactly the odd and even tests in disguise.
sin(−x) = −sin x is precisely the odd condition f(−x) = −f(x) → sin x is odd. Its graph has 180° rotation symmetry through the origin.
cos(−x) = cos x is precisely the even condition f(−x) = f(x) → cos x is even. Its graph is a mirror image across the y-axis (the peak at x = 0 sits right on the mirror).
sin x is odd; cos x is even
8.classify · neither
Show that f(x) = x² + x and g(x) = eˣ are both neither even nor odd.

Worked solution

Background: "neither" is the most common verdict! Mixing an even term with an odd term usually wrecks both symmetries. And one single counterexample number is enough to demolish a symmetry claim.
f(−x) = (−x)² + (−x) = x² − x. Compare: f(x) = x² + x (not equal → not even) and −f(x) = −x² − x (not equal → not odd). Neither.
g(x) = eˣ: test x = 1. g(−1) = e−1 ≈ 0.37, but g(1) = e ≈ 2.72 (so not even) and −g(1) ≈ −2.72 (so not odd). Neither.
Moral: eˣ has no left-right symmetry at all — it hugs the axis on the left and explodes on the right.
Both are neither even nor odd
9.odd × even
Classify f(x) = x·cos x, then evaluate ∫−55 x cos x dx.

Worked solution

Background: products follow sign-style rules: odd × even = odd, odd × odd = even, even × even = even. Why: multiply the two f(−x) behaviors — one sign flip survives, two sign flips cancel. This turns scary-looking integrands into instant zeros.
Test directly: f(−x) = (−x)·cos(−x) = (−x)·cos x = −x cos x = −f(x). So x cos x is odd. (Matches the rule: x is odd, cos x is even, odd × even = odd.)
Odd function, symmetric interval [−5, 5] → integral is 0. No antiderivative needed (lucky, because ∫x cos x dx needs next lesson's tool!).
x cos x is odd; ∫−55 x cos x dx = 0
10.even shortcut + FTC check
Evaluate ∫−22 x² dx with the even-function shortcut, then verify by running the FTC straight across from −2 to 2.

Worked solution

Background: for an even function the two halves of the region are mirror twins, so you may compute the right half (from 0 to a, usually easier numbers) and double it. Both routes must agree — a free self-check.
Shortcut: x² is even, so ∫−22 x² dx = 2·∫02 x² dx = 2·[x³/3]02 = 2·(8/3) = 16/3.
FTC straight across: [x³/3]−22 = 8/3 − (−8/3) = 16/3 ✓. Same answer.
16/3
11.odd shortcut + FTC check
Evaluate ∫−11 x⁵ dx by symmetry, and verify with the FTC.

Worked solution

Background: any odd power of x is an odd function, so over a symmetric interval its integral dies instantly. The FTC confirms it: an even-power antiderivative gives the same value at both endpoints, and same minus same is zero.
x⁵ is odd (odd power), interval [−1, 1] is symmetric → integral = 0.
FTC check: antiderivative x⁶/6. Evaluate: (1)⁶/6 − (−1)⁶/6 = 1/6 − 1/6 = 0 ✓.
0
12.given values · combo
Given ∫17 f dx = 6, find ∫71 4f dx.

Worked solution

Background: two rules chain together here: first slide the constant 4 out front, then fix the backwards limits with a sign flip. Do the rules one at a time and nothing can go wrong.
Constant out: ∫71 4f dx = 4·∫71 f dx.
Reversal: ∫71 f dx = −∫17 f dx = −6.
Combine: 4·(−6) = −24.
−24
13.no computation needed
Explain why ∫−ππ sin x dx = 0 without computing any antiderivative. Then verify with the FTC anyway.

Worked solution

Background: "explain why no computation is needed" is a genuine AP question style. The expected answer names the symmetry: odd function + symmetric interval. One good sentence beats a page of algebra.
Explanation: sin x is odd and [−π, π] is symmetric about 0, so the area below the axis on [−π, 0] exactly cancels the equal area above the axis on [0, π]. Net: 0.
FTC verification: antiderivative −cos x. At π: −cos π = −(−1) = 1. At −π: −cos(−π) = −(−1) = 1. Integral = 1 − 1 = 0 ✓.
0, by odd symmetry (confirmed by FTC)
14.mixed integrand
Evaluate by splitting term-by-term and using the right rule on each: (a) ∫−22 (x³ + 3x² + 2) dx   (b) ∫−11 (5x⁹ − 2x³ + 7) dx.

Worked solution

Background: linearity lets you handle each term separately: odd terms vanish on symmetric intervals, even terms get doubled from 0 to a, constants make simple rectangles. Most of the integrand often just disappears.
(a) x³ odd → 0.   3x² even → 2·∫02 3x² dx = 2·[x³]02 = 2·8 = 16.   Constant 2 → 2 × (width 4) = 8. Total: 0 + 16 + 8 = 24.
Check (a) by straight FTC: antiderivative x⁴/4 + x³ + 2x. At 2: 4 + 8 + 4 = 16. At −2: 4 − 8 − 4 = −8. 16 − (−8) = 24 ✓.
(b) 5x⁹ and −2x³ are both odd → both contribute 0. Constant 7 → 7 × (width 2) = 14. Total: 14.
Check (b): antiderivative x¹⁰/2 − x⁴/2 + 7x. At 1: ½ − ½ + 7 = 7. At −1: ½ − ½ − 7 = −7. 7 − (−7) = 14 ✓.
(a) 24   (b) 14
15.comparison sense-check
Without computing, explain (a) why ∫03 (x² + 1) dx cannot be negative, and (b) why it must be bigger than ∫03 x² dx = 9. Then (c) compute it and confirm both predictions.

Worked solution

Background: comparison facts are your error alarms. Non-negative function → non-negative integral (you can't accumulate junk and end up below zero). Taller function → bigger integral (a taller graph traps more area).
(a) x² + 1 ≥ 1 > 0 for every x, so the integral — an accumulation of positive contributions — must be positive. (In fact it's at least the 3-wide, 1-tall rectangle: at least 3.)
(b) x² + 1 > x² at every x, so its area over [0, 3] beats the area under x², which is 9.
(c) Compute: ∫03 (x² + 1) dx = [x³/3 + x]03 = 9 + 3 = 12. Indeed 12 > 0 ✓ and 12 > 9 ✓.
(c) 12 — positive and bigger than 9, as predicted
16.even + given value
A function f is even, and ∫04 f dx = 6. Find (a) ∫−44 f dx and (b) ∫−40 f dx.

Worked solution

Background: for an even function the region left of the y-axis is a mirror copy of the region on the right — so the left half holds exactly the same signed area as the right half. That single picture answers both parts.
(a) Even shortcut: ∫−44 f dx = 2·∫04 f dx = 2·6 = 12.
(b) The left half equals the right half: ∫−40 f dx = ∫04 f dx = 6. (Check with splitting: 6 + 6 = 12, the answer to (a) ✓.)
(a) 12   (b) 6
17.given values · multi-step
Given ∫05 f dx = 12, ∫35 f dx = 5, and ∫03 g dx = 4, find (a) ∫03 f dx and (b) ∫03 (2f − 3g) dx.

Worked solution

Background: multi-step given-value problems are little algebra puzzles: use splitting to find the missing piece first, then feed it into linearity. Write each rule on its own line and the puzzle solves itself.
(a) Split: ∫05 = ∫03 + ∫35 → 12 = ∫03 f dx + 5 → ∫03 f dx = 7.
(b) Linearity: ∫03 (2f − 3g) dx = 2·∫03 f dx − 3·∫03 g dx = 2·7 − 3·4 = 14 − 12 = 2.
(a) 7   (b) 2
18.average value tie-in
(a) Find the average value of f(x) = x³ on [−2, 2]. (b) Explain why the average value of any odd function on [−a, a] is 0, and what that means about the graph.

Worked solution

Background: average value = (1/(b − a))·∫ab f dx — the integral spread evenly over the interval's width (Lesson 14). If the integral in the numerator is 0, the average is 0, no matter how wild the function looks.
(a) Average = (1/(2 − (−2)))·∫−22 x³ dx = (1/4)·0 = 0, since x³ is odd on a symmetric interval.
(b) For any odd f on [−a, a], the integral is 0 by the cancellation picture, so average = (1/(2a))·0 = 0. Meaning: the function spends its time above and below zero in perfectly balanced fashion — every positive height is matched by an equal negative height at the mirror point.
(a) 0   (b) integral is 0 by odd symmetry, so average = 0
19.spot the error
A student computes: "∫−33 x³ dx = 2·∫03 x³ dx = 2·(81/4) = 81/2." Find the error, give the correct answer, and verify it with the FTC.

Worked solution

Background: the doubling shortcut belongs to EVEN functions only. Using it on an odd function is the classic symmetry mistake — it doubles the right half instead of letting the left half cancel it. Always classify the function before picking a shortcut.
The error: x³ is odd, not even, so "double the right half" is illegal here. The left half is the negative of the right half.
Correct: odd function on symmetric interval [−3, 3] → integral = 0.
FTC check: [x⁴/4]−33 = 81/4 − 81/4 = 0 ✓. (The student's ∫03 x³ dx = 81/4 was right — only the doubling step was wrong.)
Correct value: 0 — the doubling rule only applies to even functions
20.challenge · all rules at once
Suppose f is odd with ∫06 f dx = 5, and g is even with ∫06 g dx = 11. Find: (a) ∫−66 f dx   (b) ∫−66 g dx   (c) ∫−66 (3g − 2f) dx   (d) ∫−60 f dx   (e) ∫6−6 g dx

Worked solution

Background: this is the full toolkit in one problem — odd/even shortcuts, linearity, splitting, and reversal. Take the parts one rule at a time; each is a one-liner once you name the rule being used.
(a) f odd on [−6, 6] → ∫−66 f dx = 0.
(b) g even → ∫−66 g dx = 2·∫06 g dx = 2·11 = 22.
(c) Linearity: 3·(22) − 2·(0) = 66.
(d) Split (a): 0 = ∫−60 f dx + ∫06 f dx = ∫−60 f dx + 5 → ∫−60 f dx = −5. (Makes sense: an odd function's left half is the negative mirror of its right half.)
(e) Reversal: ∫6−6 g dx = −∫−66 g dx = −22.
(a) 0   (b) 22   (c) 66   (d) −5   (e) −22
