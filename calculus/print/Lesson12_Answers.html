ANSWER KEY — Integration Lesson 12 Test: Motion II — Acceleration & Gravity

For the grader · full working shown · give credit for correct method with small arithmetic slips · g = 10 m/s² throughout

1. A sled starts from rest and accelerates at a steady 5 m/s². Write v(t), and find the velocity after 6 seconds.
Work: velocity = starting velocity + integral of acceleration: v(t) = 0 + ∫₀ᵗ 5 du = 5t. Then v(6) = 5 × 6 = 30. Units: (m/s²) × s = m/s. Answer: v(t) = 5t; v(6) = 30 m/s.
2. A skateboarder is already moving at 4 m/s and accelerates at 3 m/s². Write v(t) and find the velocity after 2 seconds.
Work: change in velocity = ∫₀ᵗ 3 du = 3t; add the start: v(t) = 4 + 3t. Then v(2) = 4 + 6 = 10. (Check t = 0: v = 4 ✓.) Answer: v(t) = 4 + 3t; v(2) = 10 m/s. (Losing the starting 4 is the common error.)
3. A ball is dropped from a hot-air balloon. After 4 seconds, how fast is it moving and how far has it fallen?
Work: dropped means v₀ = 0. Integrating a = 10 gives v = 10t; integrating again gives distance fallen = 5t². v(4) = 40; s(4) = 5 × 16 = 80. Answer: 40 m/s, having fallen 80 meters.
4. A coin is dropped from a 45-meter rooftop. How long to hit the ground, and how fast when it lands?
Work: distance fallen = 5t², so set 5t² = 45 → t² = 9 → t = 3 s. Landing speed: v = 10t = 10 × 3 = 30. (Check: 5 × 3² = 45 ✓.) Answer: 3 seconds, landing at 30 m/s.
5. An object's velocity is v(t) = 2 + 6t m/s. Use an integral to find how far it travels in the first 3 seconds.
Work: distance = ∫₀³ (2 + 6t) dt = [2t + 3t²]₀³ = (6 + 27) − 0 = 33. Units: (m/s) × s = m. Answer: 33 meters.
6. A ball is thrown straight up at 40 m/s. Find the peak time, peak height, and landing time.
Work: v(t) = 40 − 10t. Peak when v = 0: t = 4 s. Peak height = ∫₀⁴ (40 − 10t) dt = [40t − 5t²]₀⁴ = 160 − 80 = 80 m. Lands when s(t) = 40t − 5t² = 5t(8 − t) = 0 → t = 8 s (double the peak time, as expected). Answer: peak at t = 4 s, height 80 m, lands at t = 8 s.
7. A car moving at 30 m/s brakes with acceleration −6 m/s². How long to stop, and how far while stopping?
Work: v(t) = 30 − 6t; stops when 30 − 6t = 0 → t = 5 s. Distance = ∫₀⁵ (30 − 6t) dt = [30t − 3t²]₀⁵ = 150 − 75 = 75 m. (Triangle check: ½ × 5 × 30 = 75 ✓.) Answer: stops in 5 seconds, after 75 meters.
8. Acceleration staircase: a = 2 from t = 0 to 3, then a = 4 from t = 3 to 5; starting velocity 5 m/s. Find v(5).
Work: area under the acceleration graph = change in velocity. Rectangle 1: 2 × 3 = 6 m/s. Rectangle 2: width 5 − 3 = 2 (NOT 5), so 4 × 2 = 8 m/s. Total change = 14; v(5) = 5 + 14 = 19. Answer: 19 m/s. (Common error: width 5 for the second rectangle → 26, wrong; or forgetting the starting 5 → 14, wrong.)
9. Derive v = v₀ + at by integrating a constant acceleration a.
Work: acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, so the CHANGE in velocity from 0 to t is ∫₀ᵗ a du = [au]₀ᵗ = at (a rectangle of height a, width t). An integral only gives the change, not the actual velocity — the speedometer already read v₀ at the start — so the true velocity is start + change: v = v₀ + at. Full credit needs: the integral, the value at, and a reason for adding v₀. Answer: v = v₀ + at, with v₀ added because the integral gives only the CHANGE in velocity.
10. Rocket from rest: a = 8 m/s² for 3 s, then coasts at constant velocity for 5 s. Velocity after phase 1 and total distance over all 8 seconds?
Work: Phase 1: v(t) = 8t → v(3) = 24 m/s. Phase 1 distance = ∫₀³ 8t dt = [4t²]₀³ = 36 m. Phase 2: coasting means a = 0, velocity stays 24 m/s → distance = 24 × 5 = 120 m. Total = 36 + 120 = 156 m. Answer: 24 m/s after phase 1; total distance 156 meters.
