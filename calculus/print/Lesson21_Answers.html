ANSWER KEY — Integration Lesson 21 Test: Completing the Power Rule

For the grader · full working shown · give credit for correct method with small arithmetic slips · missing "+ C" on an indefinite integral costs the point only once per test

1. Rewrite as a power of x, then integrate: ∫ 1/x³ dx. Check by differentiating.
Work: 1/x³ = x−3. New power: −3 + 1 = −2. So x−2/(−2) = −1/(2x²). Check: d/dx[−½x−2] = −½·(−2)x−3 = x−3 ✓. Answer: −1/(2x²) + C. (Classic error: dividing by the OLD power −3, giving x−2/(−3) — the differentiation check exposes it.)
2. Integrate: ∫ x1/4 dx.
Work: new power: 1/4 + 1 = 5/4. Divide by 5/4 = multiply by 4/5: x5/4/(5/4) = (4/5)x5/4. Check: (4/5)(5/4)x1/4 = x1/4 ✓. Answer: (4/5)x5/4 + C. (Classic error: writing (5/4)x5/4 — multiplying instead of dividing by the new power.)
3. Integrate: ∫ 12/x⁴ dx.
Work: 12/x⁴ = 12x−4. Then 12 · x−3/(−3) = −4x−3 = −4/x³. Check: d/dx[−4x−3] = −4·(−3)x−4 = 12x−4 ✓. Answer: −4/x³ + C. (Watch for a dropped minus sign — the most common slip with negative powers.)
4. Integrate: ∫ (√x + 4) dx.
Work: term by term: ∫ x1/2 dx = x3/2/(3/2) = (2/3)x3/2; ∫ 4 dx = 4x. Check: (2/3)(3/2)x1/2 + 4 = √x + 4 ✓. Answer: (2/3)x3/2 + 4x + C. (Classic error: forgetting the constant term integrates to 4x, not 4.)
5. Split the fraction, then integrate: ∫ (x⁴ + 5)/x² dx.
Work: (x⁴ + 5)/x² = x⁴/x² + 5/x² = x² + 5x−2. Then x³/3 + 5·x−1/(−1) = x³/3 − 5/x. Check: d/dx[x³/3 − 5x−1] = x² + 5x−2 ✓. Answer: x³/3 − 5/x + C. (Grader note: trying to integrate top and bottom separately — x⁵/5 over x³/3 — is a conceptual error worth discussing; there is no "quotient rule" for integrals.)
6. Evaluate exactly: ∫13 2/x² dx.
Work: antiderivative of 2x−2 is 2·x−1/(−1) = −2/x. Then [−2/x]13 = (−2/3) − (−2/1) = −2/3 + 2 = 4/3. Answer: 4/3. (Classic error: mangling the double negative to get −8/3 or 2/3 — insist on writing the subtraction out.)
7. Evaluate exactly: ∫09 √x dx.
Work: antiderivative (2/3)x3/2. At 9: 93/2 = (√9)³ = 27, so (2/3)·27 = 18. At 0: 0. Difference: 18 − 0. Answer: 18. (Classic error: computing 93/2 as 9·1.5 = 13.5 — remind them it means (√9)³.)
8. Evaluate exactly: ∫49 1/√x dx.
Work: 1/√x = x−1/2; antiderivative x1/2/(1/2) = 2√x. Then [2√x]49 = 2·3 − 2·2 = 6 − 4 = 2. Answer: 2. (Sanity check for grader: region is 5 wide, heights between 1/3 and 1/2, so answer must be between 5/3 and 5/2 — 2 fits.)
9. A student writes ∫ 1/x² dx = ln(x²) + C. Explain the error and give the correct answer.
Work: ln is ONLY the antiderivative of 1/x (the power −1, where the power rule divides by zero). For any other power — here 1/x² = x−2 — the power rule works fine: x−1/(−1) = −1/x. Check: d/dx[−1/x] = 1/x² ✓, while d/dx[ln(x²)] = 2/x ✗. Answer: −1/x + C; ln is reserved for the single power n = −1.
10. Sap flows at r(t) = 6/√t liters per hour, t from 1 to 4 hours. (a) Rewrite r(t) as a power of t. (b) Total liters collected.
Work: (a) r(t) = 6t−1/2. (b) Total = ∫14 6t−1/2 dt; antiderivative 6·2√t = 12√t (check: d/dt[12t1/2] = 6t−1/2 ✓). [12√t]14 = 12·2 − 12·1 = 24 − 12 = 12. Units: (L/hr)·hr = liters. Answer: (a) 6t−1/2   (b) 12 liters. (Award (a) even if (b) fails; award (b) method credit if the antiderivative 12√t is right but arithmetic slips.)
