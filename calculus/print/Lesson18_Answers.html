ANSWER KEY — Integration Lesson 18 Test: Substitution II — u-Substitution

For the grader · full working shown · give credit for correct method with small arithmetic slips · missing "+ C" costs half a point

1. For ∫ 2x(x²+4)⁶ dx, state u and du.
Work: u is the inside of the power: u = x²+4. Then du = (derivative of u) dx = 2x dx. Bonus observation: the 2x factor is present exactly, so u-substitution will work. Answer: u = x²+4, du = 2x dx.
2. Compute ∫ 2x(x²−3)⁴ dx.
Work: (1) u = x²−3. (2) du = 2x dx — exact match. (3) Swap: ∫ u⁴ du. (4) Integrate: u⁵/5. (5) Back: (x²−3)⁵/5 + C. (6) Check: derivative = 5(x²−3)⁴ · 2x / 5 = 2x(x²−3)⁴ ✓. Answer: (x²−3)⁵/5 + C.
3. Compute ∫ 3x²(x³+5)³ dx.
Work: u = x³+5, du = 3x² dx — exact match. Swap: ∫ u³ du = u⁴/4. Back: (x³+5)⁴/4 + C. Check: 4(x³+5)³ · 3x² / 4 = 3x²(x³+5)³ ✓. Answer: (x³+5)⁴/4 + C.
4. Compute ∫ cos x (sin x)⁴ dx.
Work: u = sin x, du = cos x dx — exact match. Swap: ∫ u⁴ du = u⁵/5. Back: (sin x)⁵/5 + C. Check: 5(sin x)⁴ · cos x / 5 = cos x (sin x)⁴ ✓. Answer: (sin x)⁵/5 + C.
5. Compute ∫ x(x²+1)³ dx.
Work: u = x²+1, du = 2x dx. We only have x dx — off by the constant 2, so borrow: x dx = du/2. Swap: ∫ u³ · (du/2) = ½ · u⁴/4 = u⁴/8. Back: (x²+1)⁴/8 + C. Check: 4(x²+1)³ · 2x / 8 = x(x²+1)³ ✓. Answer: (x²+1)⁴/8 + C. (Common error: forgetting the borrow and writing (x²+1)⁴/4.)
6. Compute ∫ x²(x³−4)³ dx.
Work: u = x³−4, du = 3x² dx, so x² dx = du/3. Swap: ∫ u³ · (du/3) = ⅓ · u⁴/4 = u⁴/12. Back: (x³−4)⁴/12 + C. Check: 4(x³−4)³ · 3x² / 12 = x²(x³−4)³ ✓. Answer: (x³−4)⁴/12 + C.
7. Does u-substitution work on ∫ (x³+2)⁵ dx?
Work: candidate u = x³+2 needs du = 3x² dx, so the integral would need an x² factor (up to a constant). There is NO x factor of any kind, and only constants may be borrowed — you can never divide by 3x². So u-substitution fails. The legal fallback: expand (x³+2)⁵ into a polynomial and integrate term by term with the power rule. Answer: No — the derivative of the inside (3x²) does not appear as a factor; expand and integrate term by term instead.
8. Is ∫ x(x²+7)² dx = (x²+7)³/6 + C correct?
Work: differentiate the claim. d/dx of (x²+7)³/6: power rule brings down the 3 → 3(x²+7)²/6 = (x²+7)²/2; chain rule multiplies by the inside's derivative 2x → (x²+7)² · 2x / 2 = x(x²+7)². That matches the integrand exactly. Answer: Yes, the classmate is right — the derivative gives back x(x²+7)² ✓.
9. Compute ∫₀¹ 4x(x²+1) dx.
Work: u = x²+1, du = 2x dx, so 4x dx = 2 du. Indefinite: ∫ u · 2 du = 2 · u²/2 = u². Back: (x²+1)². Check: derivative = 2(x²+1) · 2x = 4x(x²+1) ✓. FTC: at x = 1: (1+1)² = 4; at x = 0: (0+1)² = 1; subtract: 4 − 1 = 3. Answer: 3.
10. Water flows in at r(t) = 2t(t²+1) L/min. How much flows in from t = 0 to t = 2?
Work: total = ∫₀² 2t(t²+1) dt. u = t²+1, du = 2t dt — exact match. Indefinite: ∫ u du = u²/2 → back: (t²+1)²/2. Check: derivative = 2(t²+1) · 2t / 2 = 2t(t²+1) ✓. Plug in: at t = 2: (4+1)²/2 = 25/2; at t = 0: (0+1)²/2 = 1/2; subtract: 25/2 − 1/2 = 24/2 = 12. Answer: 12 liters.
