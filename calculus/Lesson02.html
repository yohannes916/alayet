Lesson 2 — Area Under a Graph = the Total

Triangles, Trapezoids, and What Area Really Means · Intuitive Calculus for a Curious Student · Lesson 2 of 40

📚 All lessons← Lesson 1Next: Lesson 3 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: in Lesson 1 the rate graph was made of flat steps, so the area under it was rectangles. Today the rate graph gets slanted — and the amazing news is that "area under the rate graph = the total" STILL works. You'll learn to handle slanted lines with triangles and trapezoids, and to read what an area means from its units.

1 Quick recap of Lesson 1 — total = area of rectangles

  • Integration answers ONE question: "I know the rate at every moment — what's the TOTAL?"
  • For a steady rate, total = rate × time. On the graph, that's a rectangle: width = time, height = rate, and width × height = the total.
  • When the rate changed in steps, we chopped the trip into steady pieces and added the rectangle areas.
  • So the big picture rule so far: the total accumulated = the area under the rate graph. Today we test that rule on graphs that aren't flat.

2 A slanted line makes a triangle — and area still means total

  • Suppose a car speeds up smoothly from 0: at every moment it's a bit faster than the moment before. Its speed graph is a straight slanted line starting at 0 and climbing to some top speed v.
  • The region under that line isn't a rectangle — it's a triangle: flat along the bottom (the time axis), straight up the right side (the final speed), and the slanted speed line as the top.
  • Where does ½ × base × height come from? Take a rectangle that's base wide and height tall, and slice it corner-to-corner with a diagonal. You get two identical triangles — each one is exactly half the rectangle. So: triangle area = ½ × base × height.
speed (m/s) the rectangle (base × height) v | ┌ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ /│ cut along the diagonal... | ┆ / │ | ┆ / ◄ the triangle is | ┆ / HALF of it: ½·b·h 0 +/──────────────────────→ time (s) 0 base = time b
  • Why does the triangle's area still equal the distance? Same reason as before: think of the trip as many tiny moments; in each tiny moment, distance ≈ (speed right then) × (tiny time) — a skinny rectangle under the line. All those skinny rectangles together fill up the triangle. Add them all → the triangle's area. (Lesson 3 takes this "skinny rectangle" idea and runs with it.)

3 Worked example — speeding up from 0 to 20 m/s

Worked example: a car speeds up steadily from 0 to 20 m/s over 10 seconds. How far does it travel?

The speed graph is a slanted line from (0, 0) up to (10, 20). The region underneath is a triangle with base = 10 s and height = 20 m/s.
distance = area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 meters.

Sanity check: the car's slowest speed was 0 and fastest was 20, so its "middle" speed was 10 m/s. Traveling 10 s at that middle speed gives 10 × 10 = 100 m. ✓ Same answer — a steadily changing speed accumulates exactly like its halfway speed.

4 Trapezoids — when the rate starts above zero

  • What if the speed climbs steadily from 10 m/s to 30 m/s? The graph is still a slanted line, but it starts up at height 10, not at 0. The shape underneath is a trapezoid (flat bottom, two vertical sides of different heights, slanted top).
  • No new formula needed! Slice the trapezoid horizontally into two shapes you already know:
speed (m/s) 30 | /│ | / │ TRIANGLE on top 20 | / │ height = 30 − 10 = 20 | / │ 10 |────────────────────│ | RECTANGLE below │ height = 10 0 +─────────────────────┴──→ time (s) 0 b
  • Rectangle below: the speed was AT LEAST 10 the whole time → a rectangle of height 10 across the full width.
  • Triangle on top: the EXTRA speed grew steadily from 0 up to 30 − 10 = 20 → a triangle of height 20.
  • Total = rectangle + triangle. Over 4 seconds: 10 × 4 = 40 plus ½ × 4 × 20 = 40 → 80 meters. Every trapezoid in this course surrenders to "rectangle + triangle."

5 Units — what the area actually MEANS

  • Area under a graph always carries the unit (y-axis unit) × (x-axis unit), because area = height × width.
  • Speed graph: (meters/second) × (seconds) = meters. The seconds cancel — area is a distance.
  • Water graph: (liters/minute) × (minutes) = liters. Area is an amount of water.
  • Money graph: (dollars/day) × (days) = dollars. Area is money earned.
  • Pattern: a rate's unit is always "something per time," so the time units cancel and the area under a rate graph always carries the TOTAL's unit. If someone hands you a rate graph, you instantly know what its area means.

6 Preview — what about real curves?

  • Rectangles handle flat graphs. Triangles and trapezoids handle straight slanted graphs. But nature loves curves — a falling skydiver, a cooling cup of cocoa, a growing colony of bacteria. A curved graph fits NO simple shape from geometry class.
  • Next lesson's trick: chop the curve into skinny slices and pretend each slice is flat — a staircase of rectangles that hugs the curve. That idea is the doorway to real integration.
✏️ Exercises — 20 problems (write your answer on paper, then reveal)
1.rectangle area
A pump pushes water into a fish tank at a steady 5 liters per minute for 8 minutes. On the rate graph this is a flat line at height 5. What is the area of the rectangle under it, and how much water entered the tank?

Worked solution

Background: a steady rate draws a flat line on the graph. The region under it is a rectangle: width = how long the rate lasted, height = the rate. The rectangle's area IS the total accumulated — that was the big discovery of Lesson 1.
Width = 8 minutes, height = 5 liters/minute.
Area = 8 × 5 = 40. Units: (liters/minute) × minutes = liters.
Area = 40 → 40 liters of water entered
2.reading widths
A car drives at a steady 50 miles per hour from t = 1 hour to t = 4 hours. How far does it travel during that stretch? (Careful: the width of the rectangle is NOT 4.)

Worked solution

Background: when a graph runs "from t = a to t = b," the width of the region is b − a (end minus start), not just b. Forgetting to subtract the start time is one of the most common mistakes in all of calculus — the interval starts at 1, not at 0.
Width = 4 − 1 = 3 hours. Height = 50 mph.
Distance = area = 3 × 50 = 150 miles.
150 miles
3.triangle area
A go-kart starts from rest (speed 0) and speeds up steadily to 10 m/s over 4 seconds. Its speed graph is a slanted line. How far does it travel?

Worked solution

Background: "starts from rest and speeds up steadily" makes a slanted line from (0, 0) up to the top speed. The region under it is a triangle — half of the rectangle you'd get if the kart had gone top speed the whole time. Triangle area = ½ × base × height, and that area is the distance.
Base = 4 seconds, height = 10 m/s.
Distance = ½ × 4 × 10 = ½ × 40 = 20. Units: seconds × meters/second = meters.
20 meters
4.triangle area
A sprinter accelerates from 0 to 8 m/s during the first 2 seconds of a race, speeding up steadily. How many meters does she cover in those 2 seconds?

Worked solution

Background: a sprinter out of the blocks is a classic speeding-up-from-zero situation. She does NOT cover 8 × 2 = 16 m — she was only at 8 m/s for the final instant! The slanted speed line makes a triangle, which is exactly half of that too-big rectangle.
Base = 2 s, height = 8 m/s.
Distance = ½ × 2 × 8 = 8 meters. (Exactly half of the wrong answer 16.)
8 meters
5.money · reading widths
You walk dogs for $6 per hour. On a graph of your earning rate, there's a flat line at height 6 from hour 3 of your day to hour 7. What is the area under that stretch, and what does it tell you?

Worked solution

Background: money accumulates at a rate too — dollars per hour. Area under an earning-rate graph = dollars earned, because (dollars/hour) × hours = dollars. And remember: width = end − start.
Width = 7 − 3 = 4 hours. Height = 6 dollars/hour.
Area = 4 × 6 = 24. Units: (dollars/hour) × hours = dollars.
Area = 24 → you earned $24 during those 4 hours
6.what does area mean?
A graph shows a faucet's flow rate: the y-axis is in liters per minute, and the x-axis is in minutes. Without computing anything, answer: what UNIT does the area under this graph carry, and what does the area MEAN in real life?

Worked solution

Background: area = height × width, so its unit is always (y-axis unit) × (x-axis unit). For any rate graph, the rate's "per minute" cancels against the minutes on the x-axis, leaving the unit of the thing that accumulated. You can know what an area means before doing any arithmetic.
Unit of area = (liters/minute) × (minutes) = liters — the minutes cancel.
Meaning: the total number of liters of water that came out of the faucet during that time.
Unit: liters. Meaning: the total water delivered.
7.falling object
A rock is dropped off a cliff. Its speed grows steadily from 0 to 30 m/s during the first 3 seconds of falling (gravity speeds things up by about 10 m/s every second). How far does the rock fall in those 3 seconds?

Worked solution

Background (physics): gravity is nature's steadiest accelerator — every second of falling adds about 10 m/s of speed. So a dropped object's speed graph is a perfect slanted line from 0, and the distance fallen is the triangle underneath it.
Base = 3 s, height = 30 m/s.
Distance = ½ × 3 × 30 = ½ × 90 = 45 meters.
45 meters
8.trapezoid
A train is already moving at 10 m/s when it starts speeding up steadily, reaching 30 m/s after 4 seconds. Split the region under its speed graph into a rectangle plus a triangle, and find the distance traveled.

Worked solution

Background: when the speed starts above zero, the region under the slanted line is a trapezoid. Slice it horizontally at the starting speed: below the slice is a rectangle (the speed the train ALWAYS had), above it is a triangle (the EXTRA speed it gained, growing from 0).
Rectangle: height 10, width 4 → area = 10 × 4 = 40 m.
Triangle: height 30 − 10 = 20, base 4 → area = ½ × 4 × 20 = 40 m.
Total = 40 + 40 = 80 meters.
80 meters
9.trapezoid · water
A garden hose's flow rate rises steadily from 4 liters per minute to 10 liters per minute over 6 minutes. How much water comes out in total?

Worked solution

Background: a rising flow rate makes a slanted line starting at height 4 — a trapezoid underneath. Rectangle + triangle handles it: the hose delivered AT LEAST 4 L/min the whole time (rectangle), plus an extra amount that grew from 0 to 6 L/min (triangle).
Rectangle: 4 × 6 = 24 liters.
Triangle: extra rate grows 0 → (10 − 4) = 6, so ½ × 6 × 6 = 18 liters.
Total = 24 + 18 = 42 liters.
42 liters
10.reverse · find the height
A car accelerates from rest with a straight-line speed graph, and in 10 seconds it covers exactly 50 meters. What top speed did it reach at the end of the 10 seconds?

Worked solution

Background: usually we know the graph and find the area. Here it's backwards: we know the area (the distance, 50 m) and the base (10 s), and we solve for the triangle's height (the top speed). Same formula, different unknown.
Set up: ½ × base × height = distance → ½ × 10 × h = 50.
That's 5 × h = 50, so h = 50 ÷ 5 = 10.
Check: ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 ✓.
Top speed = 10 m/s
11.money · area meaning
A lemonade stand earns money at a steady rate of $20 per day. On the earning-rate graph, find the area under the line from day 2 to day 5, and explain in words what that number means.

Worked solution

Background: two skills in one: reading the width from "from day a to day b" (width = b − a), and knowing what the area means (dollars, because (dollars/day) × days = dollars). The area is never just a number — it always stands for a real total.
Width = 5 − 2 = 3 days. Height = 20 dollars/day.
Area = 3 × 20 = 60.
Meaning: the stand earned $60 during days 2 through 5.
Area = 60 → $60 earned between day 2 and day 5
12.braking triangle
A cyclist going 16 m/s hits the brakes and slows steadily to a complete stop over 8 seconds. Her speed graph is a slanted line going DOWN from 16 to 0. How far does she travel while braking?

Worked solution

Background: a triangle doesn't care which way it slants! Slowing steadily from 16 to 0 makes a downhill line, and the region under it is still a triangle — base 8, height 16 (the height is at the START now instead of the end). Area = distance, exactly as before. This is real physics: it's how crash investigators compute stopping distances.
Base = 8 s, height = 16 m/s.
Distance = ½ × 8 × 16 = ½ × 128 = 64 meters.
64 meters
13.weather trapezoid
During a storm, the rainfall rate rises steadily from 2 millimeters per hour to 6 millimeters per hour over 4 hours. How many millimeters of rain fall in total? (Split into rectangle + triangle.)

Worked solution

Background (weather): rainfall rate (mm/hour) is how fast the water level in a rain gauge rises; total rainfall (mm) is what the gauge reads afterward. A steadily strengthening storm makes a slanted rate line starting at 2 — a trapezoid. Rectangle (the rain that was always falling) + triangle (the extra as the storm grew).
Rectangle: 2 mm/h × 4 h = 8 mm.
Triangle: extra rate grows 0 → (6 − 2) = 4, so ½ × 4 × 4 = 8 mm.
Total = 8 + 8 = 16 mm.
16 millimeters of rain
14.reverse · find the rate
A tank gains 90 liters of water in 6 minutes from a pump running at a perfectly steady rate. On the rate graph this is a rectangle of width 6 whose area is 90. How tall is the rectangle — that is, what was the pump's rate?

Worked solution

Background: area = width × height, so if you know the area and the width, the height is area ÷ width. In rate language: rate = total ÷ time. This is the "derivative direction" — recovering the rate from the total — and it's how you'd read a pump's speed off a water meter.
height = area ÷ width = 90 ÷ 6 = 15.
Check: 15 L/min × 6 min = 90 L ✓.
15 liters per minute
15.biology triangle
A bacteria colony starts growing slowly, then faster: its growth rate climbs steadily from 0 new bacteria per hour to 40 new bacteria per hour over 5 hours. How many new bacteria appear during those 5 hours?

Worked solution

Background (biology): a growth rate ("new bacteria per hour") is a rate like speed or water flow — it just counts organisms instead of meters or liters. The total number of NEW bacteria is the area under the growth-rate graph: (bacteria/hour) × hours = bacteria. A steadily climbing rate from 0 makes a triangle.
Base = 5 hours, height = 40 bacteria/hour.
New bacteria = ½ × 5 × 40 = ½ × 200 = 100.
100 new bacteria
16.reverse trapezoid
A motorcycle moving at 10 m/s speeds up steadily for 6 seconds and covers 90 meters during that time. What speed does it reach at the end? (Hint: rectangle + triangle, but now the triangle's height is the unknown.)

Worked solution

Background: the trapezoid decomposition works backwards too. The rectangle part (the starting speed held the whole time) is known, so subtract it from the total distance; whatever distance is left over must have come from the triangle of extra speed. Then solve the triangle for its height.
Rectangle: 10 m/s × 6 s = 60 m. Distance left for the triangle: 90 − 60 = 30 m.
Triangle: ½ × 6 × (extra speed) = 30 → 3 × (extra speed) = 30 → extra speed = 10 m/s.
Final speed = starting 10 + extra 10 = 20 m/s. Check: 10×6 + ½×6×10 = 60 + 30 = 90 ✓.
20 m/s
17.compare shapes
Car A speeds up steadily from 0 to 20 m/s over 10 seconds. Car B drives at a constant 10 m/s for the same 10 seconds. Compute both distances. What do you notice, and why does it make sense?

Worked solution

Background: a steadily changing rate accumulates exactly the same total as a constant rate equal to its halfway value — because the triangle (½ × b × h) has the same area as a rectangle of half the height (b × h/2). This "average rate" idea gets its own lesson later (Lesson 14).
Car A: triangle, ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 m.
Car B: rectangle, 10 × 10 = 100 m.
They tie! Car A spent half the time below 10 m/s and half above, and the slow start exactly cancels the fast finish. Halfway between 0 and 20 is 10 — Car B drove Car A's average speed.
Both travel 100 m — a steady 10 m/s matches the average of 0-to-20
18.energy trapezoid
A bakery's electricity use (power) climbs steadily from 2 kilowatts to 4 kilowatts over 3 hours as the ovens heat up. How much energy, in kilowatt-hours, does the bakery use? (Power in kW is the RATE of using energy; energy in kWh is the TOTAL.)

Worked solution

Background (physics): power is to energy what speed is to distance: power (kilowatts) is how FAST you're using energy, and energy (kilowatt-hours) is the total used — it's literally the unit on your family's electric bill. Area under a power graph = kW × hours = kWh, the energy.
Rectangle: 2 kW × 3 h = 6 kWh.
Triangle: extra power grows 0 → (4 − 2) = 2 kW, so ½ × 3 × 2 = 3 kWh.
Total = 6 + 3 = 9 kWh.
9 kilowatt-hours
19.challenge · piecewise graph
A city bus makes this trip: cruises at 10 m/s for 20 seconds, then speeds up steadily from 10 to 20 m/s over the next 10 seconds, then cruises at 20 m/s for 30 seconds. Sketch the speed graph (flat, slanted, flat), break the region under it into rectangles and a triangle, and find the total distance.

Worked solution

Background: real trips are piecewise: flat stretches (constant speed) and slanted stretches (speeding up or slowing down). The master strategy: cut the graph at every place its shape changes, find each piece's area with rectangle or rectangle-plus-triangle, and add. Every piece's area is that piece's distance.
Piece 1 (flat at 10, width 20): 10 × 20 = 200 m.
Piece 2 (slanted 10 → 20, width 10): rectangle 10 × 10 = 100 m, plus triangle ½ × 10 × (20 − 10) = 50 m → 150 m.
Piece 3 (flat at 20, width 30): 20 × 30 = 600 m.
Total = 200 + 150 + 600 = 950 m.
950 meters
20.challenge · full journey
A subway train leaves a station, speeds up steadily from 0 to 15 m/s over 10 seconds, holds 15 m/s for 40 seconds, then brakes steadily from 15 m/s to 0 over 20 seconds, stopping at the next station. How far apart are the two stations?

Worked solution

Background: this triangle–rectangle–triangle shape is the true speed profile of almost every subway ride you've ever taken: accelerate, cruise, brake. The distance between stations is the whole area under it. Notice the braking triangle counts distance too — the train is still moving forward while slowing down!
Speed-up triangle: ½ × 10 × 15 = 75 m.
Cruise rectangle: 15 × 40 = 600 m.
Braking triangle: ½ × 20 × 15 = 150 m.
Total = 75 + 600 + 150 = 825 m.
825 meters between stations
