Lesson 6 — The Definite Integral: the Official Symbol

Naming the Thing We've Been Doing All Along · Intuitive Calculus for a Curious Student · Lesson 6 of 20

📚 All lessons← Prev: Lesson 5Next: Lesson 7 →
🎯 Goal of this lesson: meet the official symbol ∫ab f(x) dx, learn what every piece of it means, and get comfortable reading it, writing it, and evaluating it with plain geometry. No new math today — just a beautiful new name for the thing you've been doing since Lesson 1.

1 Time to name the thing we've been doing

  • For five lessons you've been answering the same question over and over: "what is the TOTAL accumulated from a to b?" Total distance from hour 0 to hour 3. Total water from minute 2 to minute 9. Total area under the rate graph between two moments.
  • Mathematicians got tired of writing that whole sentence, so about 350 years ago they invented a compact symbol for it. Here it is, the star of the course:
  • ∫ab f(x) dx
  • This is called a definite integral — "definite" because it has a definite start (a) and a definite end (b), so it comes out to be one definite number.
  • Nothing about the IDEA is new. You already know what this means. Today you just learn to read and write it like a pro.

2 Anatomy of the symbol, piece by piece

upper limit → b ⌠ "add up..." → ⎮ f(x) dx ← "...a tiny bit of x" ⌡ ↑ lower limit → a the integrand (the rate / height)
  • ∫ — this is a stretched-out letter S, and it stands for Sum. It's the "add everything up" command. (Chop–multiply–add from Lesson 1, remember?)
  • a and b — the numbers at the bottom and top are the limits of integration: a is where you start adding, b is where you stop. "Limits" here just means "boundaries," nothing scary.
  • f(x) — the function in the middle is called the integrand. It's the rate — the HEIGHT of the graph at each spot.
  • dx — read it as "a tiny little bit of x." It's the infinitely skinny WIDTH of each slice. In Lesson 3 our rectangles had widths like 1, then 0.5, then 0.25... dx is what the width becomes when the slices get infinitely skinny.
  • Put it all together and the whole symbol literally reads: "add up rectangles that are f(x) tall and dx skinny, from x = a to x = b." Height × width, summed. That's all it says.
Reading practice: ∫29 r(t) dt reads "add up r(t)-tall, dt-skinny slices from t = 2 to t = 9" — in plain English, the total amount accumulated between time 2 and time 9 when the rate is r(t). (When the variable is time we write t and dt instead of x and dx — same idea, different letter.)

3 What it equals: the EXACT area

  • In Lesson 3 you sandwiched the true area between too-small and too-big staircases of rectangles, and watched the sandwich squeeze tighter as the rectangles got skinnier.
  • ∫ab f(x) dx is defined to be the number those estimates are closing in on: the EXACT area under the graph of f from a to b.
  • Not an approximation. Not "close enough." The definite integral is the perfect, exact answer that infinitely many infinitely skinny rectangles would give.
  • So from now on, "find ∫ab f(x) dx" and "find the exact area under f from a to b" and "find the exact total accumulated from a to b" are three ways of saying the same thing.

4 Evaluating with pure geometry (no shortcuts yet)

  • We don't have the magic shortcut yet (that's Lesson 7!). But when the graph is made of straight lines, we can compute integrals EXACTLY with shapes you already know.
  • Constant integrand → rectangle. ∫14 5 dx: the graph of 5 is a flat line at height 5, from x = 1 to x = 4. That's a rectangle: width = 4 − 1 = 3, height = 5. Area = 5 × 3 = 15.
  • Line through the origin → triangle. ∫04 3x dx: the graph of 3x starts at height 0 and rises to height 3 × 4 = 12 at x = 4. The region underneath is a triangle: base = 4, height = 12. Area = ½ × 4 × 12 = 24.
height height 5 | ┌─────────┐ 12 | /│ | │ │ | / │ | │ 15 │ | / │ | │ │ | / 24 │ | │ │ | / │ 0 +--+---------+--→ x 0 +/─────────+──→ x 0 1 4 0 4 ∫₁⁴ 5 dx = 5×3 = 15 ∫₀⁴ 3x dx = ½·4·12 = 24
  • Line that starts above zero → trapezoid (or rectangle + triangle). ∫13 (x+1) dx: at x = 1 the height is 2; at x = 3 the height is 4. The region is a trapezoid. Two ways to get its area:
    • Slice it: a rectangle 2 tall and 2 wide (area 4) with a triangle on top, base 2, height 4 − 2 = 2 (area ½ × 2 × 2 = 2). Total = 4 + 2 = 6.
    • Trapezoid formula: average of the two parallel sides × width = ½(2+4) × 2 = 6. Same answer, of course.
Worked example: evaluate ∫05 2x dx by geometry. The line 2x goes from height 0 (at x = 0) up to height 10 (at x = 5). Triangle: base 5, height 10, area = ½ × 5 × 10 = 25.

5 Two friendly properties (with pictures)

  • Splitting: ∫ab f(x) dx = ∫ac f(x) dx + ∫cb f(x) dx for any point c between a and b. Why? Chop the region under the graph with a vertical cut at x = c. The left piece plus the right piece is obviously the whole thing. A road-trip version: miles from 9 AM to noon + miles from noon to 5 PM = miles from 9 AM to 5 PM.
| _.-·˙˙·-._ | .·˙ ┊ ˙·. | left ┊ right | piece ┊ piece +---+-------+------+---→ x a c b whole area (a to b) = left (a to c) + right (c to b)
  • Zero width: ∫aa f(x) dx = 0. Start at a and stop at... a. The "region" is a line with no width at all, so it encloses no area. In trip language: how far do you travel between 3 PM and 3 PM? Nowhere. Zero.

6 Units and meaning: what kind of number comes out

  • A definite integral isn't just a number — it carries units, and they follow the height × width rule.
  • If f is measured in liters per minute and x is in minutes, then each slice is (liters/minute) × (minutes) = liters. The whole integral is in liters — a total amount of water, not a rate.
  • General rule: units of the integral = (units of f) × (units of x). The "per" always cancels: miles per hour × hours = miles; dollars per day × days = dollars.
  • This is a great sanity check. If someone claims ∫010 v(t) dt is a speed, they've made a mistake — integrating a rate always gives back a total.

1.words → symbol
Water flows into a tank at a rate of r(t) liters per minute. Write the definite integral that means "the total water that flowed in from minute 2 to minute 9."

Worked solution

Background: a definite integral has four parts — the ∫ sign ("add up"), the lower limit (start), the upper limit (end), the integrand (the rate function), and the little d ("tiny bit of the variable"). To translate a sentence, just find the start, the end, and the rate.
Start = minute 2 → lower limit is 2. End = minute 9 → upper limit is 9.
The rate is r(t), and the variable is t, so the tiny width is dt.
∫29 r(t) dt
2.symbol → words
A car's speed after t hours is v(t), measured in miles per hour. Translate ∫05 v(t) dt into a plain-English sentence.

Worked solution

Background: reading an integral out loud: "add up v(t)-tall, dt-skinny slices from t = 0 to t = 5." Each slice is speed × a tiny bit of time = a tiny bit of distance. Adding up all the tiny bits of distance gives the total distance.
The limits say: from time 0 to time 5 hours.
The integrand v(t) is a speed, so each little piece v(t) × dt is a little piece of distance.
The total distance (in miles) the car travels between t = 0 and t = 5 hours.
3.name the parts
In the integral ∫16 (2x + 3) dx, identify: (a) the integrand, (b) the lower limit, (c) the upper limit, (d) what the dx means.

Worked solution

Background: vocabulary check! The integrand is the function being added up (the height of the graph). The limits of integration are the start and end values on the x-axis. The dx is the infinitely skinny width of each slice.
(a) The integrand is 2x + 3 — the function sandwiched between the ∫ and the dx.
(b) The lower limit is 1 (the small number at the bottom — where the adding starts).
(c) The upper limit is 6 (the number at the top — where the adding stops).
(d) dx means "a tiny little bit of x" — the width of each infinitely skinny rectangle.
(a) 2x + 3   (b) 1   (c) 6   (d) the tiny slice width
4.geometry · rectangle
Evaluate ∫26 4 dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: when the integrand is a constant, its graph is a flat horizontal line, and the region under it is a plain rectangle. Width = (upper limit − lower limit), height = the constant. A definite integral of a constant is just width × height.
Width = 6 − 2 = 4. Height = 4.
Area = 4 × 4 = 16.
∫26 4 dx = 16
5.geometry · rectangle
Evaluate ∫07 3 dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: same idea as the last problem — a constant integrand makes a rectangle. Don't let the lower limit of 0 bother you: the width is still (end − start).
Width = 7 − 0 = 7. Height = 3.
Area = 3 × 7 = 21.
∫07 3 dx = 21
6.zero width
Evaluate ∫33 (x² + 100) dx — no computing allowed! Explain your answer in one sentence.

Worked solution

Background: the limits of integration are the start and the end of the region. Look closely at these limits before you touch the integrand — the region runs from x = 3 to x = 3.
Start and end are the same point, so the region has width 3 − 3 = 0.
A region with zero width encloses zero area — no matter how tall the function is. (Trip version: distance traveled between 3 PM and 3 PM is zero, even in a rocket.)
∫33 (x² + 100) dx = 0
7.geometry · triangle
Evaluate ∫05 2x dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: the graph of 2x is a straight line through the origin (at x = 0 the height is 0). Under such a line, from 0 out to some point, sits a right triangle: base = the upper limit, height = the line's height at that endpoint. Area = ½ × base × height.
At x = 5, the height is 2 × 5 = 10.
Triangle: base = 5, height = 10 → area = ½ × 5 × 10 = 25.
∫05 2x dx = 25
8.geometry · triangle
Evaluate ∫06 x dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: the integrand here is just x itself — the line of slope 1 through the origin. Its height at any point equals the x-value there. Same triangle setup as before.
At x = 6, the height is 6.
Triangle: base = 6, height = 6 → area = ½ × 6 × 6 = 18.
∫06 x dx = 18
9.geometry · trapezoid
Evaluate ∫13 (x + 1) dx by splitting the region into a rectangle plus a triangle. Then confirm with the trapezoid formula.

Worked solution

Background: the line x + 1 doesn't pass through the origin, so the region from x = 1 to x = 3 is a trapezoid (a slanted-top shape). Find the height at each end first: at x = 1, height = 1 + 1 = 2; at x = 3, height = 3 + 1 = 4. Then either slice it (rectangle + triangle) or use the trapezoid formula: ½(side 1 + side 2) × width.
Rectangle part: 2 tall, 2 wide (from x = 1 to 3) → area = 4.
Triangle on top: base 2, height 4 − 2 = 2 → area = ½ × 2 × 2 = 2.
Total = 4 + 2 = 6. Trapezoid check: ½(2 + 4) × 2 = 3 × 2 = 6 ✓.
∫13 (x + 1) dx = 6
10.splitting property
You're told ∫04 f(x) dx = 10 and ∫47 f(x) dx = 6. Find ∫07 f(x) dx.

Worked solution

Background: the splitting property says a region cut by a vertical line at x = c satisfies ∫ab = ∫ac + ∫cb. Here the pieces already fit together perfectly: one runs 0 → 4, the next runs 4 → 7, so together they cover 0 → 7 with no gap and no overlap.
∫07 f = ∫04 f + ∫47 f = 10 + 6.
∫07 f(x) dx = 16
11.splitting · solve backwards
You're told ∫08 f(x) dx = 20 and ∫03 f(x) dx = 7. Find ∫38 f(x) dx.

Worked solution

Background: splitting works in reverse too. If (whole) = (left piece) + (right piece), then (right piece) = (whole) − (left piece). Trip version: if the whole day was 20 miles and the morning was 7 miles, the afternoon was 13 miles.
∫08 f = ∫03 f + ∫38 f, so 20 = 7 + ∫38 f.
∫38 f = 20 − 7 = 13.
∫38 f(x) dx = 13
12.units
In ∫29 r(t) dt, the rate r(t) is in liters per minute and t is in minutes. What units does the value of the integral have, and why?

Worked solution

Background: every slice of an integral is height × width = (units of the integrand) × (units of the variable). Adding slices doesn't change units, so the whole integral has those same multiplied units. The "per" always cancels.
Each slice: (liters/minute) × (minutes) = liters — the minutes cancel.
A sum of liters is liters. The integral is a total amount of water, not a rate.
Liters — because (liters per minute) × (minutes) = liters
13.words → symbol → number
A hiker walks at a steady v(t) = 3 miles per hour. (a) Write the integral meaning "total distance from hour 1 to hour 4." (b) Evaluate it with geometry.

Worked solution

Background: two skills in one — translating (start, end, rate → limits and integrand) and then evaluating. Since the rate is the constant 3, the region under the graph is a rectangle, and this whole integral is secretly the rate × time formula from Lesson 1 wearing its fancy new symbol.
(a) Start 1, end 4, rate 3: ∫14 3 dt.
(b) Rectangle: width = 4 − 1 = 3 hours, height = 3 mph → area = 3 × 3 = 9. Units: mph × hours = miles.
(a) ∫14 3 dt   (b) 9 miles
14.geometry · trapezoid
Evaluate ∫25 2x dx using geometry. (Careful — this triangle-looking problem doesn't start at 0!)

Worked solution

Background: the graph of 2x IS a line through the origin, but our region only runs from x = 2 to x = 5 — so the piece we want is a trapezoid, not a full triangle. Heights at the ends: at x = 2 the line is at 2 × 2 = 4; at x = 5 it's at 2 × 5 = 10. Trapezoid area = ½(side 1 + side 2) × width.
Left height = 4, right height = 10, width = 5 − 2 = 3.
Area = ½(4 + 10) × 3 = ½ × 14 × 3 = 21.
(Another way: big triangle 0→5 is ½ × 5 × 10 = 25; cut away the small triangle 0→2, which is ½ × 2 × 4 = 4. Left over: 25 − 4 = 21 ✓ — that's the splitting property in action!)
∫25 2x dx = 21
15.geometry · trapezoid
Evaluate ∫04 (2x + 1) dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: the line 2x + 1 starts at height 1 (not 0!) when x = 0, so even starting from x = 0 the region is a trapezoid. Step one is always the same: find the height at each end, then use ½(side 1 + side 2) × width.
At x = 0: height = 2(0) + 1 = 1. At x = 4: height = 2(4) + 1 = 9.
Width = 4 − 0 = 4. Area = ½(1 + 9) × 4 = ½ × 10 × 4 = 20.
(Slicing check: rectangle 1 × 4 = 4, plus triangle ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 → 4 + 16 = 20 ✓.)
∫04 (2x + 1) dx = 20
16.words → symbol → number
Water pours from a hose at a steady 6 liters per minute. (a) Write the definite integral for the total water from minute 3 to minute 10. (b) Evaluate it and give the units.

Worked solution

Background: the full translate-then-evaluate pipeline. The rate is the constant 6 (liters per minute), the clock runs from 3 to 10, and a constant integrand always makes a rectangle: width × height.
(a) ∫310 6 dt.
(b) Width = 10 − 3 = 7 minutes, height = 6 L/min → 6 × 7 = 42. Units: (liters/minute) × minutes = liters.
(a) ∫310 6 dt   (b) 42 liters
17.splitting · missing piece
A function f satisfies ∫010 f(x) dx = 30 and ∫610 f(x) dx = 12. Find ∫06 f(x) dx, and say which property you used.

Worked solution

Background: the splitting property ∫010 = ∫06 + ∫610 is really just "whole = left + right." When you know the whole and one piece, subtract to get the other piece. This time the UNKNOWN is the left piece.
30 = ∫06 f + 12.
∫06 f = 30 − 12 = 18.
∫06 f(x) dx = 18 (splitting property, chopping at x = 6)
18.symbol → words · units
A house uses electricity at the rate p(t) kilowatts, where t is measured in hours after midnight. Translate ∫024 p(t) dt into plain English, including what units the answer carries. (Reminder from Lesson 1: a kilowatt is a rate of energy use — kilowatt-hours of energy per hour.)

Worked solution

Background (physics): power (kilowatts) is the RATE of using energy, and energy (kilowatt-hours) is the TOTAL — the thing the electric company bills you for. Integrating a rate over a time window always gives the total accumulated in that window, with units (rate units) × (time units).
Limits 0 to 24 hours = midnight to midnight, one full day.
Each slice: kilowatts × hours = kilowatt-hours. So the integral is a total energy.
The total energy the house uses in the whole day, measured in kilowatt-hours
19.geometry · downhill line
Evaluate ∫04 (6 − x) dx using geometry.

Worked solution

Background: nothing in the trapezoid method cares whether the line goes uphill or downhill. The graph of 6 − x starts high and slopes DOWN — but the recipe is identical: height at each end, then ½(side 1 + side 2) × width. (The line would hit zero at x = 6, but we stop at x = 4, so the whole region is still above the axis.)
At x = 0: height = 6 − 0 = 6. At x = 4: height = 6 − 4 = 2.
Width = 4. Area = ½(6 + 2) × 4 = ½ × 8 × 4 = 16.
∫04 (6 − x) dx = 16
20.challenge · piecewise graph
A machine's output rate f(x) (parts per hour, x in hours) follows this graph: it holds steady at 5 from x = 0 to x = 2, then drops along a straight line from 5 (at x = 2) down to 0 (at x = 6). Evaluate ∫06 f(x) dx and say what the number means.

Worked solution

Background: a piecewise graph is made of simple shapes glued together, and the splitting property lets us handle each piece separately: ∫06 = ∫02 + ∫26. The flat part makes a rectangle; the straight-line drop to zero makes a triangle.
Piece 1 (flat): rectangle from x = 0 to 2, height 5 → area = 5 × 2 = 10.
Piece 2 (ramp down): triangle from x = 2 to 6, base = 6 − 2 = 4, height 5 → area = ½ × 4 × 5 = 10.
Total = 10 + 10 = 20. Units: (parts/hour) × hours = parts.
∫06 f(x) dx = 20 — the machine makes 20 parts in the 6 hours
